# UA-19 — The Physics of Place: Infrasound, Archaeoacoustics and the Choice of Sacred Sites

This dossier examines the claim that "sacred sites are not randomly chosen places; since the Stone Age they have been built as **lenses**, known for their acoustic and magnetic properties." Cave paintings falling on echo zones, the ~110 Hz resonance of hypogea and the "110 Hz prefrontal shift" claim all come to the same table. Conclusion: **acoustic preference in site selection is a real pattern**; but the "110 Hz magic frequency" reading failed independent replication and rests on one small study from a single laboratory.

## 1. Intersection Map: Archaeoacoustics, Geophysics, Seismology, Neuroacoustics and Architecture Look at the Same Cave

Five lines intersect here:

1. **Archaeoacoustics** — resonance measured in caves, megaliths and hypogea.
2. **Geophysics** — natural emission/resonance measured at some sacred sites.
3. **Seismology** — comparing earthquake cycles with ritual timing (earthquake sound and temple strategies in ancient civilisations).
4. **Neuroacoustics** — the effect of low-frequency sound on the brain (especially the 110 Hz claim).
5. **Architecture and ritual** — the "hidden" physical setup of site selection.

Why do they not converge? Because converging produces a sentence that unsettles both sides: **a site may really have been chosen "acoustically" — but that is preference and experience, not frequency numerology.**

## 2. Anomaly Inventory (With Slugs)

### 2.1 Painting and echo overlap in caves

Reznikoff and Dauvois (1988, *Bulletin de la Société Préhistorique Française* 85:238–246) took measurements in several European caves and reported that **wall paintings fall on the most strongly echoing parts of the space**: the same pattern recurring in different caves weakens the coincidence hypothesis.

This is the dossier's most solid empirical piece — but note: the measurement supports the sentence **"the painters knew about sound"**; not the sentence **"sound had a special effect on the brain."** The existence of echo and the neural outcome of echo are separate questions.

### 2.2 Hal Saflieni Hypogeum and ~110 Hz

Jahn, Devereux and Ibison (1996) took in-situ measurements in the "Oracle Room" of Malta's Hal Saflieni Hypogeum and reported the room's resonance at **about 110 Hz**. This is an in-situ measurement in a single structure; it cannot be generalized, but it is valuable.

### 2.3 Cook et al. (2008): 110 Hz and prefrontal asymmetry

Cook, Pajot and Leuchter (2008, *Time and Mind* 1:95–104) presented tones from 90 to 130 Hz in 10 Hz steps to **30 adults** and recorded EEG (**this study is EEG, not fMRI** — correcting a frequent error in the literature). Finding: **at 110 Hz**, a shift of dominance from the left frontal region to the right; at 90 and 100 Hz, a shift in the opposite direction; at 120 and 130 Hz, no difference.

The status of this finding is kept low in this dossier: (1) single laboratory; (2) n = 30, small; (3) since 2008, it has **not been independently replicated**; (4) the venue is a humanities-adjacent journal (peer review at social-science rather than neuroscience standard); (5) EEG asymmetry is fragile in interpretation (hair, reference, electrode placement effects).

### 2.4 The Trieste (SBRG) replication attempt: the claim weakens

A more systematic attempt was run in the SBRG (Systemic Biomedical Research Group) line; results **did not support** the Cook finding: participant responses spread across **individual-specific frequencies** (one sensitive at 90, one at 105, one at 120 Hz); no single "sacred frequency" (110 Hz) stood out; no left-hemisphere dominance was observed; the group average clustered near ~105 Hz. This suggests the best form of the claim is **"in the person, not in the frequency"** — which itself refutes the "fixed magic frequency" reading.

### 2.5 Additional lines: Chavín, megaliths, infrasound and "earthquake hotels"

- Mirelman and Kolar (2014) measured resonance around ~120 Hz in the galleries of Peru's Chavín de Huántar; this adds one more data point to the pattern of "low-hundreds Hz resonance in sacred structures."
- "Infrasound-earthquake hotels": the story that Napoleon Bonaparte felt a subjective "shaking" in the pyramid chamber; measurements suggesting that pyramids' large limestone masses focus passing traffic vibrations (seismic-noise and building-resonance studies) — this tradition shows that **spatial acoustic focusing** is empirically traceable.
- Earthquake sound in ancient civilisations: speaking statues, oracle voices and the interpretation of earthquakes as the "voice of god" in funerary cults are recorded ethnographically; but there is no controlled architectural-plan measurement involving seismometers — this goes into the speculative-record box.

### 2.6 The boundary between science and numerology

A low-tier publication (on Zenodo) proposed a "frequency hierarchy" linking 110 Hz to golden-ratio (φ) series. This dossier records it as **notable but low-tier**: φ-based acoustic hierarchies are hierarchies assumed before measurement; the proposition that 110 Hz uniquely fits φ can only be shown by independent, double-blind measurements. The κ-catalysis/similarity results in the HZ-06 dossier (Ismail et al., 2018) further weaken the φ share of this low-tier publication.

## 3. Three-Level Excavation

### Level 1 — Observation

What is observed: (a) cave paintings fall on the most echoing zones with meaningful overlap (confirmed in more than one cave); (b) a chamber of the Hal Saflieni Hypogeum resonates at ~110 Hz; (c) Cook et al. reported an EEG asymmetry shift at 110 Hz — EEG, single lab, unreplicated; (d) in the SBRG line, the effect spread across individual-specific frequencies; (e) ~120 Hz around in the Chavín galleries; (f) φ-numerology proposals exist and are assumptions made before measurement.

### Level 2 — Pattern

The pattern is this: **in site selection, there is acoustic preference; there is no acoustic magic.**

- **Preference is real:** People **preferred** certain spaces (cave echo zones, hypogeum chambers, megalithic courts) over others and placed painting/ritual/architecture there. This is a pattern of "discovery and preference" and it is measurable.
- **A fixed frequency is unsupported:** The best test of the 110 Hz claim showed the effect spread across **individual-specific frequencies**. So the reading "the resonance of a place is a specific magic number" falls.

A second pattern: **a sacred site is a place that offers a "good acoustic experience"** — echo, bass, sustain, isolation. What was selective was not the number but the **quality of experience.**

### Level 3 — Why is it so?

Why is place selected by experience rather than by number? Because the human auditory system is not **frequency-tagged** but **quality-tagged**: qualities such as "large, empty, sacred, eerie, alive"; it cannot measure a frequency, but it *feels* a space. So cultures built "echoing chambers", not "110 Hz chambers."

Third layer: **place enters the nervous system as an "amplifier."** When a ritual is performed in a naturally resonant room, the sound both lasts longer and touches the body more (the infrasound component); so the same ritual produces a "stronger" experience. Place is the **gain** of the ritual — not a mystical message.

## 4. HIDDEN PATTERN CANDIDATE: HZ-19 — Acoustic and Magnetic Lens (corrected: acoustic preference, not a frequency constant)

> **Candidate Law (HZ-19, corrected).** Sacred site selection follows measurable acoustic preferences: spaces with high echo time, low-hundreds-Hz resonance and infrasound richness; ritual and art settle there. But the effect is **in the person and the quality, not in the frequency**; fixed "magic frequency" readings such as 110 Hz failed independent replication.

Simplified statement:

**T_place ≈ P(echo time, low-hundreds-Hz richness, isolation)** &nbsp;&nbsp; and &nbsp;&nbsp; **∂(effect)/∂(fixed frequency) ≈ 0** (on current evidence)

**Three predictions:**
1. **Trace of preference:** Acoustic measurements of sites counted as sacred (echo time, resonance peak frequencies, bass richness) should differ **systematically** from randomly chosen neighbouring sites — the preference should be acoustically visible.
2. **Personal peak, not fixed number:** In the same space, people's "my hair stood up" responses should attach not to one frequency but to **individual peak frequencies** and to the space's overall resonance envelope.
3. **Echo time is the strongest predictor:** The physical measure that best predicts perceived "sacredness" should be **echo time and bass sustain**, not peak frequency (consistent with HZ-06's "harmonic isolation" idea).

**Discriminating test (HZ-19/1 — sample, preregistered).** Measurements in at least 20 sites classified as "sacred" and "not sacred": echo time (RT60), bass response curve, infrasound level + visitors' subjective and EEG measures. Prediction: the sacred classification loads on echo time/bass richness, not peak frequency; 110 Hz is not special.

**Second test (HZ-19/2 — 110 Hz, double-blind).** 110 Hz, the individual resonance frequency, and a matched control frequency, in balanced order, double-blind, with a power-analyzed sample (n ≥ 80). Prediction: the 110 Hz arm differs from the individual peak neither subjectively nor in EEG; the Cook finding is not replicated.

**Third test (HZ-19/3 — archive).** Cave painting locations and acoustic maps re-measured in different caves and by independent researchers; the echo-painting overlap modelled **together with** alternative predictors such as painting density, light and ease of access. Prediction: the echo variable remains significant when light and access are controlled.

## 5. Evidence Thermometer: 🟡 / 🟠 / 🔴

**Why 🟡?** The cave-painting/echo overlap has been observed in multiple caves; the Hal Saflieni and Chavín measurements are in-situ and concrete. But the sample is at the level of "a few caves + a few structures"; there is no blinding, preregistration or power analysis; ethnographic interpretations are free.

**Why 🟠 (neural frequency specificity)?** Cook et al. is EEG (not fMRI), n = 30, single lab, no independent replication in 17 years; the SBRG line showed the effect lies in the person, not the frequency.

**Why 🔴 (numerical mysticism)?** The chain "110 Hz = φ = sacredness" is an assumed hierarchy, not a measurement; no special relation of 110 Hz to φ has been demonstrated.

> **What would remain if falsified?** If HZ-19/2 shows 110 Hz really does separate from other frequencies, the "no fixed frequency" sentence falls and the Cook finding gains weight. In the opposite direction: if the cave studies are not reproduced in independent measurements, what remains is still the **acoustic preference of sacred sites** (echo time, bass sustain) — a useful piece of architectural knowledge in itself.

## 6. Practical Takeaway and Gateway Bridge

1. **The honest promise:** Place is a real gate: echo, bass, isolation and directionality determine the experience of sound. Acoustic architecture measurably benefits ritual experience.
2. **App bridge:** The app's "place" layer (e.g. focused-place practice, silence instructions, room-choice suggestions) can rest on real physics: a quiet place, plus for app audio a "sustained, low-frequency rich" room — not "sing/chant/shout into an echo."
3. **Group practice.** In group ritual, the shared room's resonance amplifies the experience — but that does not mean the group forms a secret "frequency" bond; the amplification comes from the **sustain time and bass texture** of the sound.
4. **Caution.** Infrasound (below 20 Hz) exposure can produce nausea, dizziness, blurred vision and panic in experimental settings; this is not a "mysterious sacred force" but a vegetative stimulation effect. Do not enter environments claiming "high infrasound" as an experiment.
5. **Research note:** This dossier connects with UA-18 (rhythm), HZ-06 (harmonic isolation), HZ-24 (sleep anchor) and HZ-25 (place); the Φ-2 synthesis will test whether the "place gain" idea can enter all programme protocols (sound, rhythm, sleep, Ganzfeld) as a common multiplier.
