# UA-40 — The Measurement Problem and Boundary Physics: Where Is the Observer in the Equation?

This dossier examines the oldest gap in quantum mechanics — that "measurement" is not defined inside the theory — and answers one question honestly: does this gap say anything about consciousness, or does it remain an accounting problem internal to physics? The conclusion is unexpectedly two-sided: **at the boundary of physics the observer does have a place — but its name is not consciousness, it is the irreversible record.** At the same time, whether collapse is itself physical stays open not as a philosophical dispute but as a window narrowing in the laboratory.

## 1. Intersection Map: Quantum Foundations, Information Theory and Philosophy of Mind Look at the Same Gap

Four lines intersect here:

1. **Quantum foundations** — the measurement problem, collapse models, the family of interpretations (Copenhagen, many-worlds, Bohm, relational, QBism).
2. **Experimental quantum optics** — Bell tests, delayed choice, the quantum eraser, macroscopic superposition experiments.
3. **Information theory** — irreversibility, records, entropy, no-signalling theorems.
4. **Philosophy of mind and neuroscience** — from Wigner to Stapp, the claim that "consciousness triggers collapse"; Orch OR and the anesthesia experiments.

Why do they not merge? Because merging them produces sentences that satisfy neither side. Say "observer" to a physicist and she means the apparatus; say "observer" to a philosopher and he means the experience. **What this dossier finds is that these two meanings have historically been conflated in a systematic way** — and once the conflation is undone, what remains is a much smaller open window than commonly assumed, but not a zero one.

## 2. Anomaly Inventory (with citations)

### 2.1 The gap inside the theory: "measurement" undefined

The standard axioms of quantum mechanics describe two different evolutions: (i) **unitary, reversible** Schrödinger evolution for isolated systems; (ii) an **non-unitary, probabilistic** projection upon measurement. But the theory never says where the boundary between them lies. The von Neumann chain makes this plainest: if the detector too counts as a quantum system, unitary evolution seems valid at every link of the chain; nothing inside the theory fixes the point where "collapse" enters. Heisenberg called that boundary the **"Schnitt"** (cut) and conceded it was an operational shortcut only.

Schrödinger's cat (1935) displays this gap in its sharpest form: "the cat is both dead and alive" is of course not a claim about nature but a demonstration of where the seam between the theory's language and the world sits.

### 2.2 Wigner's friend and "whose fact is a fact?"

Wigner (1961) suggested the Schrödinger chain could be extended all the way to a *conscious* observer: if the friend has seen the outcome, the cat is now "definite"; but if the friend is herself treated as a quantum system, then for Wigner outside the lab the superposition persists. This generates the question of whose fact a fact is.

Its modern form became a theorem: Frauchiger & Renner (2018, *Nature Communications* 9:3711), in "Quantum theory cannot consistently describe the use of itself", constructed a scenario in which the inferences of four agents contradict one another. The theorem shows that at least one of three assumptions must be false — (a) agents trust their own outcomes ("Q"), (b) single-world realism ("S"), (c) quantum theory applies to agents too ("C"). Frauchiger–Renner is an **accounting theorem**; it says nothing about the role of consciousness — but it exposes the price of every crude realism that tries to make facts independent of any observer.

Experimental forms exist as well: Proietti et al. (2019, *Science Advances* 5:eaaw9832) performed an experimental test of "local observer independence"; Bong et al. (2020, *Nature Physics* 16:1199–1204) proved a strong no-go theorem for the Wigner's friend paradox and showed in a Bell-type test that one of the assumptions must give way. The important detail: in these experiments the "observer" is not a human being but **quantum systems equipped with measurement devices** — consciousness again stays out of the equation.

### 2.3 Bell tests: locality closed, the gap moved elsewhere

The Bell (1964) inequalities made local hidden variables testable. Clauser (1972), Aspect et al. (1981–82) followed; in 2015 three independent groups (Hensen et al., *Nature* 526:682; Giustina et al., *Physical Review Letters* 115:250401; Shalm et al., *Physical Review Letters* 115:250402) performed **loophole-free** Bell tests. The 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics went to Clauser, Aspect and Zeilinger.

The right reading: **local hidden variables are eliminated.** The wrong reading: "action at a distance" or "everything is connected." The no-signalling theorem fixes mathematically that correlations **cannot carry a signal**: local statistics are independent of what is done at the distant end. Entanglement therefore is not a "channel"; and because it is not a channel, it offers no easy escape hatch for consciousness–matter interaction claims.

**Superdeterminism** (Hossenfelder & Palmer, 2020, *Frontiers in Physics* 8:139) is a technical escape route; its price is high: it abandons the assumption that grounds experimental randomness while also surrendering the experimenter's freedom of design. The dossier records it as "open but costly."

### 2.4 Decoherence: the disappearance of interference explained, the "single outcome" not

The decoherence theory of Zeh (1970) and Zurek (1981; review 2003, *Reviews of Modern Physics* 75:715) showed that a quantum system's entanglement with its environment **sweeps out interference terms** (off-diagonal elements of the density matrix). This explains the emergence of classical appearance: in which basis classicality arises (einselection) and why we cannot see interference in macroscopic objects.

But decoherence does not reduce the **many-branched** density matrix to a single branch; it does not settle the "and/or" problem (Zurek's term). Hence a critical distinction: decoherence **does not remove the measurement problem, it relocates it** — the gap is no longer "why does interference vanish" but "why do we see a single outcome." Experimentally, decoherence predictions (suppression of photon interference by ambient pressure/temperature, macromolecule interferometry) have been confirmed repeatedly.

### 2.5 Collapse models: the window narrowing

There are models that try to solve the measurement problem with a **real physical process**: GRW (Ghirardi, Rimini, Weber, 1986, *Physical Review D* 34:470), CSL (Pearle, Ghirardi, Rimini), Diósi–Penrose (1986/1996: gravity-related objective reduction). These are **falsifiable** models; a parameter (collapse rate and length scale) is constrained experimentally.

This period saw real closure inside that window. Donadi et al. (2021, *Nature Physics* 17:74–78), measuring 3,100 metres underground at Gran Sasso with low-background high-purity germanium detectors, **ruled out the parameter-free natural version of the Diósi–Penrose model** (lower bound R₀ ≳ 0.54 × 10⁻¹⁰ m). Another important bound came from LISA Pathfinder (Helou, Slagmolen, McClelland & Chen, 2017, *Physical Review D* 95:084054). Carlesso et al. (2022, *Nature Physics* 18:243–250) reviewed the state of non-interferometric tests: the bounds now reached invalidate the early assumption that "collapse could easily hide."

But note: **collapse with Penrose's original parameters is excluded**; modified models (colored noise, new scales) are still alive. So one cannot say "there was no collapse"; one says "its most natural form has been eliminated and the surviving models are being squeezed by new constraints." Nanoparticle interferometry (interference with more than 7,000 atoms, 2025 measurements) and space-based proposals will narrow the window further.

### 2.6 Consciousness keeps returning: from Wigner to Orch OR

Wigner's (1961) proposal and Stapp's (1993) interpretation held that collapse is triggered by consciousness. Hameroff–Penrose **Orch OR** (1994; review 2014, *Physics of Life Reviews* 11:39–78) moved collapse into quantum states bound inside microtubules: when the collective state of tubulins reaches the gravitational self-reduction threshold, it produces moments of consciousness.

The clearest objection came from Tegmark (2000, *Physical Review A* 61:4194): his estimate that warm, wet environments cut coherence lifetimes to 10⁻¹³–10⁻²⁰ seconds in the nervous system pressures any model requiring sequential quantum computation. Yet that objection (as shown in detail in UA-12) targets **long-lived local coherence**; it does not strike collective regimes (superradiance, very large chromophore networks).

Hence two findings from 2024 are critical, and both are **experimental**:
1. Babcock et al. (2024, *Journal of Physical Chemistry B* 128:4035–4046): ultraviolet superradiance of more than 10⁵ tryptophans in microtubule/centriole architectures — not coherence lifetime but collective behaviour.
2. Wiest et al. (2024, *eNeuro* 11(8), DOI 10.1523/ENEURO.0291-24.2024): a microtubule-stabilizing drug (epothilone B) **delayed anesthesia-induced unconsciousness in rats.** This is direct pharmacological evidence supporting microtubules as a target of anesthetic action.

What do these two findings prove, and what do they not? **What they prove:** quantum-sensitive cytoskeletal architecture is causally linked to unconsciousness. **What they do not prove:** that unconsciousness occurs **through quantum collapse**. The middle of the chain is still empty: whether the link architecture → collective state → anesthetic threshold is quantum or classical has not been demonstrated.

### 2.7 Delayed choice and the quantum eraser: a caution against psychological readings

In delayed-choice (Wheeler) and quantum-eraser experiments (Kim et al., 2000, *Physical Review Letters* 84:1), a "decision" appears to affect the interference pattern after the fact. This is a property of the **measurement ordering** in the experiment and is consistent with no-signalling: what matters in the end is which record exists, not who "knew" what when. A retrocausal reading of this result is defended in the literature (the subject of UA-29), but the experiments require **no human consciousness**: all delayed-choice experiments yield the same result when the deciding mechanism is a computer.

## 3. Three-Level Excavation

### Level 1 — Observation

What is observed: (a) "measurement" is undefined in quantum theory; (b) decoherence explains the loss of interference but not the single outcome; (c) collapse models have been seriously squeezed, and their most natural forms eliminated; (d) everything that measures in the laboratory — photon, detector, recording device — yields the same statistics; consciousness is not required; (e) despite this, the claim that "consciousness triggers collapse" survives in the popular literature and in some readings of the 2024 microtubule experiments.

### Level 2 — Pattern

The pattern: **the term "observer" has historically carried two distinct things on one word** — (i) the physical event in which an irreversible record forms, and (ii) the subject who has experiences. Every experiment has tried to separate what (i) does from what (ii) does, and **every time (i) has proved sufficient.** This is the outcome of a series of failed separation attempts, not a single experiment: in Bell tests the "distant observer" was a detector, not a mind; in Wigner's friend experiments the "observer" was a quantum device; in delayed choice the "choice" was software.

And the complementary pattern: **the gap itself keeps moving rather than closing.** Copenhagen's cut → decoherence's einselection → the collapse models' parameter window. At every step the gap dropped one layer down, and at no step did the term "consciousness" return.

### Level 3 — Why is it like this?

Why can physics not dispense with "observer" but can dispense with consciousness? Because the structure of quantum theory **speaks in classical terms**. Probability, "event", "outcome" — all are classical terms; the theory must be embedded in a classical world. Hence the notion of a "record" is imported from outside the theory, and the theory does not define it. Decoherence supplies the **physical mechanism** of the record (entanglement with the environment); but the **existence** of the record (a single branch) remains a fundamental assumption.

The deeper pattern beneath this — and this dossier's most important inference — is: **"observer" is not outside physics; it is inside physics' way of keeping books (its accounting).** To state its own results, the theory assumes a classical world; in that classical world, everything that happens is a record. Consciousness is not the keeper of that ledger but a system that can **read** it — and reading does not require writing.

This yields the third-layer pattern: the measurement problem stays "mysterious" because we look for the observer as a **subject**; whereas the observer's function is a **record event**, and a record event occurs everywhere in the same way, namely by entanglement with the environment. Hence "consciousness triggers collapse" is not merely unsupported; it is **unnecessary**: the only thing the theory needs is irreversibility.

## 4. HIDDEN PATTERN CANDIDATE: HZ-40 — No-Measurement Boundary Physics

> **Candidate Law (HZ-40).** The entire empirical content of the term "observer" in physics' boundary equations can be reduced to the term **irreversible record**. The contribution of consciousness to the equation has appeared in no experiment to date; but whether collapse itself is physical remains open and lives on as a window narrowing in the laboratory.

Simplified expression:

**O_observer ≡ R_irreversible &nbsp;&nbsp;(empirical equivalence)** &nbsp;&nbsp; and &nbsp;&nbsp; **Γ_decoherence ≫ λ_collapse** (at laboratory and biological scales to date)

where O is the "observer term", R an "irreversible record event", Γ_decoherence the environment-induced decoherence rate, and λ_collapse the collapse-model parameter.

**Three predictions:**
1. **Machine–human equivalence:** in the same interference experiment, whether the record is read by a human or held by a machine must be statistically **indistinguishable**. All results observed to date are consistent with this prediction.
2. **Erasure asymmetry:** what restores interference visibility is the **physical erasure** of the record, not who knows about it. Prediction: when the "knowing human" variable is manipulated (blinded design), visibility does not change.
3. **The collapse window narrows:** the allowed region of parameter space must shrink **monotonically** with new non-interferometric tests (like the DP result of Donadi 2021). Prediction: eventually either a collapse signature will be found or the parameter space will shrink to something that cannot be tied to consciousness.

**Discriminating test proposal (HZ-40/1 — Mechanical Witness).** A three-arm, pre-registered design: (A) the record is held by human observers who look at the outcome; (B) the record is held only by an automated detector/database, with humans seeing only aggregate data at the end; (C) the record is restored by delayed erasure (the quantum-eraser arm). Prediction: the **visibility distributions of the three arms are identical**; being informed about the human observer has no effect (double-blind). A deviation would be the first evidence of consciousness-specific collapse; scale (e.g. 10⁶ events) sets the statistical power.

**Second test (HZ-40/2 — collapse parameter scan).** Levitated optomechanics and nanoparticle interferometry increase the mass scale; prediction: visibility loss follows the **decoherence scaling**, not the collapse scaling. The mass–time regime where the two diverge pins the parameter space; current bounds (Donadi 2021; Helou 2017) are the starting point.

**Third test (HZ-40/3 — agent-independent facts).** An upscaled form of the Wigner's friend family: the variable "who observed the outcome" is modelled as a *system* (quantum device + classical record). Pre-registered prediction: agent-independent facts are preserved and the Frauchiger–Renner contradiction is resolved by renouncing one of the assumptions (C) — not consciousness but the theory's own scope.

## 5. Evidence Thermometer: 🟢 (nature of the boundary) / 🟡 (physical status of collapse) / 🟠 (consciousness-collapse claim)

**Why 🟢?** Three independent supports: (1) all experimental quantum-optics results are produced and replicated with mindless devices; (2) decoherence quantitatively explains the origin of classical appearance; (3) loophole-free Bell tests have eliminated local hidden variables — i.e. the picture was fixed as "correlation but no signal," not "a non-conscious link."

**Why 🟡?** A *physical* solution to the measurement problem is still open: living versions of collapse models exist and their window narrows by experiment. Moreover, theorems like Frauchiger–Renner show that the "single-world, agent-independent fact" picture is not free of charge.

**Why 🟠?** The claim that "consciousness triggers collapse" (a) has no experimental evidence; (b) had its most natural physical form (parameter-free Diósi–Penrose) eliminated by Donadi 2021; (c) still faces Tegmark's coherence-time accounting at neural scales (except for collective regimes). It is not disproven; it is **unsupported and being cornered.**

> **What remains if it is refuted?** If HZ-40/1 finds a **statistical difference** between the machine and human arms, this dossier's central negative claim collapses and "consciousness-specific collapse" gains its strongest support — that would be the program's most important experiment. In the other direction: if collapse models are fully eliminated, the measurement problem reduces to an interpretive dispute; the phrase "boundary physics" then reverts to "boundary accounting" and the rest of the dossier (irreversible-record principle, machine–human equivalence, no-signalling) survives. Whichever of the two extremes occurs, **the popular claim that "quantum creates consciousness" does not survive** — it stays false in both scenarios this dossier contemplates.

## 6. Practical Implications and the Gateway Bridge

1. **When you see the word "quantum", apply the testability test.** Is the claim checkable with a detector? If it is, either the "consciousness" term in it is redundant or that term must appear as an independent variable in the test — and to date that independent variable has never shown an effect.
2. **In the Gateway literature, quantum language is a metaphor, not a claim.** Monroe's framework is built on frequency-following and attention language; there is no "quantum" in it. Its later quantumization is a **category error** that tries to present participant experiences as physical evidence — exactly the error MP-4 exists to block.
3. **The one real bridge is built along a single line, and it is experimental.** The only serious experimental channel today for a consciousness–quantum link is cytoskeletal architecture and anesthesia (UA-12, UA-21): epothilone B delaying unconsciousness, UV superradiance, collective models. The output of that line will be measurable, not philosophical — and this dossier resists writing a philosophical victory there before those results arrive.
4. **Personal practice.** Ideas such as "quantum field" or "the observer affects the wave function" can motivate a practice; but they do not **explain** a practice's efficacy. When the effects of Gateway sessions are measured, they are measured at the psychophysiological level (HZ-17, HZ-36), not the quantum level.
