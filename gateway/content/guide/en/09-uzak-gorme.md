# Remote viewing — from Gateway to coordinate remote viewing (CRV)

Remote viewing is the practice of forming perception beyond the place where your body is — about another location, time or object. Gateway introduces it in two exercises (Freedom #2 Remote Viewing, Freedom #3 Vectors); the 1983 CIA report examines the practice in the context of the Stargate programme. This topic covers both Gateway's intuitive approach and the structured **CRV** protocol developed by the military.

## 1. Remote viewing in Gateway (Freedom #2)

**Approach:** In Focus 12 extend your perception beyond the limits of your body; hold a target (a place, a person, an event); record the first impressions: colour, texture, feeling, temperature, sound. Without searching for words, without judging.

**How (in the series)**
1. Preparation + Focus 10 + Focus 12; REBAL in place.
2. Hold the target in one sentence ("the room my mother is sitting in right now").
3. "Send" your perception in that direction — as in the Vectors exercise: direction, distance, time.
4. The impressions of the first 10–20 seconds are the most valuable: colour, texture, shape, feeling.
5. Write everything in the journal; **verify afterwards** (where possible).

**Vectors (Freedom #3):** a tool for aiming perception like a vector — in space ("north, 100 metres"), in time ("yesterday 8 a.m."), toward a person ("wherever X is"). It is kin to the coordinate logic of CRV: the clearer the "address" that defines the target, the better the perception settles on it.

## 2. What is CRV? — Ingo Swann and Stargate

**Coordinate (later Controlled) Remote Viewing** is a structured protocol developed in the 1970s–80s at Stanford Research Institute (SRI) by Ingo Swann and Hal Puthoff and used in the US military's Stargate programme. The core idea: the viewer is given only a meaningless "coordinate" (a random number); there is no information about the target (blind protocol). The viewer records perception through a staged structure. The 1985 document "Coordinate Remote Viewing Stages I–VI and Beyond" (CIA reading room) and Swann's CRV manual define that structure.

**Core concepts**
- **Signal line:** the hypothesised "carrier" by which target information reaches the viewer; a radio-broadcast metaphor.
- **Aperture:** how much of the signal gets through; narrow in the early stages (the major gestalt), widening later (detail).
- **AOL — Analytical Overlay:** the mind pasting meaning onto raw perception ("it's a bridge!"). AOL is CRV's chief enemy; when noticed, it is written down and set aside (an "AOL break").
- **Structure:** the strict order of the stages. What CRV really teaches is "command of structure" — disciplining the record, not the perception.

## 3. The six stages of CRV

[[storyboard:rv]]

1. **Stage I — major gestalt (ideogram).** The moment the coordinate is read, the hand makes a reflexive "scribble" (ideogram) on the paper — kinaesthetic, not visual. The ideogram is decoded: A (motion/feeling: "rising, angular, flat, solid…"), B (basic gestalt: "structure, water, land, mountain, life form…"). Example: a wavy scribble → "water".
2. **Stage II — sensory contact.** Sounds, smells, tastes, textures, temperatures, energies; colours. Not visual but sensory: "cold, windy, rough, grey, humming". These are raw data bits that need no analysis; AOL is lowest here.
3. **Stage III — dimension, motion, mobility.** The aperture opens; the "dimensionals" arrive: vertical, horizontal, angular, volume, mass ("tall, wide, rising"). **Aesthetic impact (AI)** — the viewer being "flooded" by the perception — marks the transition into Stage III. The viewer begins to move about the target; sketches or a "tracker" are drawn. The purpose of Stage III is command of structure.
4. **Stage IV — qualitative analytic.** Beyond physical attributes: activities at the target, objects, the emotions of people present. Data are written in an eight-column matrix: S2 (sensory), D (dimensional), AI (aesthetic), EI (emotional impact), T (tangible), I (intangible), AOL, AOL/S. This is the threshold of operational use.
5. **Stage V — specific analytic.** "Interrogating" the intangible/tangible concepts from Stage IV: taking a word and calling up the concepts attached to it. The data come not from the signal line but from "cognitrons" formed in the subconscious.
6. **Stage VI — three-dimensional modelling.** Making a model of the target in clay or a similar material; moving the hands around the model to feel "what is nearby".

## 4. Gateway and CRV — differences and the bridge

| | Gateway remote viewing | CRV |
|---|---|---|
| State | Focus 12 (Hemi-Sync supported) | Ordinary waking, light relaxation |
| Structure | Intuitive, free | Strict stages, matrix |
| Target | Known (your own choice) | Blind (coordinate only) |
| Record | Journal, image-feeling | Standard format, word by word |
| Verification | Optional | Mandatory (feedback) |

**The bridge:** Gateway teaches the opening of perception; CRV teaches the **disciplined recording** of perception. The strongest practice combines the two: enter Focus 12, then record with CRV's structure. The concept "AOL" is the technical name of Gateway's principle "don't judge, just notice".

## 5. Practice at home — the blind-target protocol

1. Have a friend (or an app) put 10 photographs in envelopes and write a random 8-digit number on each.
2. Preparation + Focus 12; REBAL.
3. Read the number; make a reflex ideogram; decode A/B.
4. Stage II sensory list; Stage III dimensionals + sketch. When an AOL comes, write "AOL — bridge" and continue.
5. Finish after 10–15 minutes; open the envelope; **score** the result (1–7: unrelated → exact).
6. Journal both the record and the score; after 20–30 trials patterns appear.

**Rules:** no pressure for results; "wrong" data are data too (they teach which AOLs you are prone to); short and frequent practice beats long and rare.

## 6. The scientific status — an honest summary

Stargate was closed in 1995; in the AIR evaluation commissioned by the CIA, Jessica Utts said "there is a statistically significant effect" while Ray Hyman said "methodological flaws can explain the result" — the scientific community still stands between those two poles today. This guide promises no miracles: it presents remote viewing as a practice **that disciplines perception and is verified by the journal**. Its value lies in the discipline of attention and recording before any claim of "knowing correctly".

## 7. Ethics

- Do not intrude on others' privacy; the principle of "permission".
- Do not base medical, legal or financial decisions on remote-viewing data.
- Share results without exaggeration; be honest with yourself when scoring.
