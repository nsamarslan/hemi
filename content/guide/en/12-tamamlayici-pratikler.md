# Complementary practices — non-Monroe techniques that strengthen Gateway

Monroe's method is complete in itself; yet a handful of practices from other traditions directly support Gateway's three weak points — inability to relax, mental chatter and missing the sleep transition. This topic describes the techniques found in the app's "Extra Practices" series. **Those that belong to Monroe and the outside practices proposed by this project are clearly separated.**

## A. From the manual (Monroe)

### 1. Energy Walk
Walking (real or mental) in Focus 12 awareness. Details: topic 7. In the app, the 15-minute mental form.

### 2. Grounding
The steps of the "Need Grounding?" section: soles of the feet, water, touch, movement, naming. Three to five minutes after every deep session. Details: topic 7.

### 3. The One-Breath Technique
Once you have experienced the entire preparatory process, the manual teaches compressing it into **a single breath**: breathe in deeply; as you exhale, call up the ECB, the affirmation, resonant tuning and the feeling of Focus 10 together. Before a meeting, on a plane, in a queue — Gateway without headphones. It requires having done the long version hundreds of times first; the shortcut is the trigger of an acquired reflex.

## B. Outside practices proposed by this project (non-Monroe)

### 4. Body Scan
**Source:** the Vipassana/Mindfulness tradition (Kabat-Zinn's MBSR). **What?** Moving attention region by region from the toes to the head, observing the sensation in each without judgement. **Contribution to Gateway:** it trains the kinaesthetic perception described in the manual's "Gateway Entry" section (tingling, weight, warmth); it replaces the question "is my body asleep?" in Focus 10 with the reflex "how does my body feel?"; it is a direct foundation for the EBT and the Body Map. **When?** If the preparatory process feels hard, 1–2 weeks before Wave I; afterwards, as a pre-session warm-up.

### 5. Box Breathing
**Source:** pranayama (sama vritti); in modern use, stress management. **What?** Four equal phases: in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. **Contribution:** quickly lowers heart rate and sympathetic activity; a 3-minute preparation for those who arrive at a session "with a racing mind". It combines with resonant tuning: a humming exhale in place of the hold phases.

### 6. Progressive Muscle Relaxation
**Source:** Edmund Jacobson, 1920s. **What?** Tensing each muscle group for 5 seconds and releasing for 10; from the feet to the face. **Contribution:** trains muscle memory for those who cannot carry out the command "relax"; markedly eases the transition into Focus 10 (body asleep). The first resort for anyone stuck in Discovery #1–2.

### 7. Loving-Kindness (Metta)
**Source:** the Buddhist metta practice. **What?** Directing good wishes to yourself, a loved one, a neutral person, a difficult person and all beings. **Contribution:** the home counterpart of Heartline and Focus 18 ("heart-centred consciousness"); emotional preparation for the "service" spirit of Lifeline/Voyager (retrieval); it reduces fear- and judgement-based AOLs (topic 9). The research support is strong (positive affect, self-compassion).

### 8. Dream Journal
**Source:** the lucid-dreaming literature (LaBerge). **What?** Noting the dream in a few sentences on waking, before moving; marking recurring themes. **Contribution:** the feedback channel for Exploration Sleep, Mission Night, the Lucid Dreaming series and Conditions A–D; as recall increases, night explorations stop being "empty". The app's journal can be filtered by the "dreamlike" tag.

### 9. WBTB (Wake-Back-to-Bed)
**Source:** LaBerge and later lucid-dreaming research. **What?** After 4–6 hours of sleep stay awake for 15–30 minutes (dim light, a little reading), then go back to bed with intent. **Contribution:** uses the early-morning window of high REM density; the most productive time window for First-Stage Separation and Conditions C–D. Once or twice a week is enough; don't fragment your sleep.

### 10. Evening Review
**Source:** the Stoic evening examination (Seneca), the Pythagorean tradition; the modern gratitude journal. **What?** Rewinding the day: what happened, what I felt, what I learned, what I am grateful for. **Contribution:** completes the closing loop with Mission Day and Make Your Day (H-Plus); feeds the journaling habit; gathers the mind before sleep.

## C. How to combine them

| Need | First | Then |
|---|---|---|
| I can't relax | Progressive Relaxation → Box Breathing | Discovery #1–2 |
| The mind won't quieten | Box Breathing → Body Scan | ECB + resonant tuning → Focus 10 |
| I fall asleep | Move the session to morning instead of noon; Stay Awake (H-Plus) | Try Wave I seated |
| Night exploration is empty | Dream Journal (2 weeks) | WBTB + Exploration Sleep |
| Fear, judgement | Loving-Kindness (10 min) | Release and Recharge |
| Groggy on return | Grounding | Evening Review |

## D. Limits

- These practices are **add-ons** to Monroe's method; they do not replace the series and are not recommended by the manual.
- Techniques involving breath retention (Box Breathing) require a physician's approval in pregnancy, cardio-pulmonary conditions and panic disorder.
- Loving-kindness practice can release intense emotion in people with a trauma history; start short.

## How to use this in the app

- Catalogue → Extra Practices. The non-Monroe sessions are marked "Complementary practice (non-Monroe)" in the card description.
- Session lengths are 5–20 minutes; they require no prerequisites in the Program tab.
- Visual pack: "Body Map" (the scan line) for the Body Scan, "Portal" (breath rhythm) for the breathing practices, "Energy Flow" for Loving-Kindness.
