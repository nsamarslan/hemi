# UA-27 — The Mind-to-Mind Pool: Ganzfeld, Dream Telepathy and Twin Studies

This file examines the question "is there a shared pool between minds" along three lines of evidence: the **ganzfeld archive** (target guessing under sensory homogenisation), **dream telepathy** (sleep-laboratory records), and **twin studies** (the oldest evidence pool for claims of closeness). The verdict has three parts: (1) **the archives contain a small but stubborn deviation spread over decades**, and that deviation changes with the very method that produces it; (2) **prospective tests of the deviation have failed** — in particular Bem's 2011 pre-registered, replicable experiments collapsed with results that triggered social psychology's replication crisis; (3) the honest reading is: **the effect does not behave like a "pool" but like a "tuned receiver"** — tied to laboratory, pair and expectancy terms. This is neither "definitely exists" nor "definitely does not"; it is a **measured method-effect**, and its most serious legacy in the history of science is psi's own replication crisis.

> **Banner:** Horizon Excavation · file UA-27 · Batch 11 · Evidence Thermometer: 🟡 (archive deviation: decades-wide, small) / 🟠 (prospective replication: failed) / 🔴 (mechanism: carrier undefined) · Candidate law: HZ-27 Tuned-Receiver Interaction — **revised form: not a pool but a pair+state+experimenter interaction** · Connected layers: UA-07 (LST window), UA-28 (mind-to-matter), UA-26 (channel-mandatory sharing), HZ-30 (epistemic immunity), MP-2, Φ-2

## 1. Intersection Map: Experimental Psi, Sleep-Laboratory Archives, Behavioural Genetics and Meta-Analysis All Look at the Same "Pool"

Four lines cross here:

1. **Experimental psi** — ganzfeld, free response, autoganzfeld; meta-analysis and Bayesian re-analyses.
2. **Sleep-laboratory archives** — Maimonides (1960s–70s) and later REM telepathy studies.
3. **Behavioural genetics and twin research** — MZ/DZ comparisons, twins reared apart, heritability studies.
4. **Meta-science** — the replication crisis, pre-registration, publication bias, the decline effect.

Why do they not merge? Because merging produces a sentence both sides dislike: **the psi archives exhibit not a supernatural pool but a method-effect; and the scientific value of that effect lies not in the phenomenon but in the methodology.**

## 2. The Anomaly Inventory (Documented)

### 2.1 Ganzfeld: an archive of 40+ years and the structure of the dispute

**Protocol:** the participant is placed in sensory homogenisation (eyes half-covered, white noise), a "sender" focuses on a target; the participant describes the target verbally and an independent judge matches it. Chance level is 25% (one of four targets).

- **The positive line:** Bem & Honorton (1994, *Psychological Bulletin*) reported a 32.2% hit rate across 11 autoganzfeld studies. Storm & Ertel (2001) reconstructed the result by adding 11 studies Milton & Wiseman had missed. **Tressoldi & Storm (2020–2021, *F1000Research*, Stage-1/Stage-2 Registered Report)** pooled all ganzfeld data from 1974–2020: **effect size about 0.08 (95% CI 0.04–0.12)**; for the 2008–2018 subset, 0.133 (0.06–0.18). A transparency note worth keeping: the registered report was published with **2 approvals and 1 non-approval** in peer review — even the field's flagship meta-analysis is contested.
- **The negative line:** **Milton & Wiseman (1999, *Psychological Bulletin* 125:387–391)** analysed 30 studies from 1987–1997: standardised effect size **0.013**, statistically non-significant (Stouffer z = 0.70; p = 0.24) — it does not meet the "replicable method" criterion.
- **Bayesian re-analysis:** Rouder, Morey & Province (2013) examined the data with Bayes factors and argued that studies using **computerised randomisation** showed smaller effects; Storm and colleagues rejected this as "faulty study classification". The core of the dispute lies here: **the same archive yields effects between 0.013 and 0.13 depending on database-construction rules.**
- **The field's own model was tested:** the "noise-reduction model" predicts that the effect should grow as isolation improves. Storm & Tressoldi (2020), under the title "Ten Years On", reported: **the effect did not grow over the decade.** Within the positive camp's own framework, this is a model failure and it must be recorded honestly.

### 2.2 Bem 2011: the eight significant experiments that broke social psychology

**Bem (2011, *JPSP* 100:407–425)** reversed time across nine experiments (N > 1,000): well-established psychology tasks were run with stimulus selection made **after** the participant's response. Result: **eight of nine experiments significant**, mean effect d = 0.22 — the same size as effects routinely published in social psychology. The author opened all materials to ease replication; a follow-up meta-analysis (Bem, Utts & Johnson 2011) pooled **90 experiments from 33 laboratories in 14 countries** and reported a combined effect "exceeding the bar for decisive evidence".

The collapse chain:

- **Ritchie, Wiseman & French (2012) — "Failing the Future":** replication of Bem's experiments 8 and 9 was negative. The journal (JPSP) refused to publish "mere replications" — the beating heart of the **publication-bias engine**.
- **Galak, LeBoeuf, Nelson & Simmons (2012, *JPSP* 103:933) — "Correcting the Past: Failures to Replicate Psi":** seven experiments, **N = 3,289**, same procedure; result **d = 0.04, no different from zero.** In the meta-analysis of all replication attempts the average effect moved to zero.
- **Wagenmakers et al. (2011) — "Failing the Future":** a Bayesian analysis concluding the original evidence was inadequate.
- **The other view:** by Shermer's count, 72 of the 90 experiments showed no effect and 18 were significant; the positive camp replies that the decisive factor is not the number of experiments but **pre-registered fidelity to method**. The two sides' common ground is this: **Bem's effect, despite open materials, was not replicated.**

### 2.3 Dream telepathy: the oldest laboratory line

- **Maimonides (Ullman & Krippner, 1964–1970s):** participants awakened in REM sleep gave dream reports that were matched to the sender's target picture; most of ten studies reported significant results. **Hansel's methodological criticism:** the target envelope was opened in front of other people and the principal experimenter could freely communicate with the subject; there was no blindness. **Belvedere & Foulkes** found **chance level** in an independent replication. **Watt & Wiseman's sleep-laboratory replication** (several nights, ~20 participants) was entirely negative. Sherwood & Roe (2003) and Storm et al. (2017) meta-analyses reported positive results; Alcock's assessment is "the data are extremely messy" and "lack of replication is rampant".

### 2.4 Twin studies: strongest narrative, weakest evidence

- **Survey level:** in the Cherkas (King's College) survey, about one in five identical twins and one in ten fraternal twins reported "telepathic experiences". This is a **report rate**, not a performance measure — MZ twins have more similar narratives because they live more similar lives.
- **Controlled tests:** Blackmore (1993, "ESP and Thought Concordance in Twins") attributed high concordance to **similar thought patterns**. In the Eaves & Last study, twins could not predict their partner's opinions above chance. In the first round, "preference-based" matching scored high, but when the experimenter chose the target, the rate fell to chance — shared preference was producing what looked like "telepathy".
- **The genetic explanation:** Bouchard et al. (1990, Minnesota Study of Twins Reared Apart): MZ twins raised apart are about as similar (personality, interests, IQ, attitudes) as those raised together. In MZ twins, the heritability of EEG patterns has been reported at 76–91%. "Thinking the same thing at the same moment" can therefore be the **independent output of the same wiring.**
- **TV experiments (Playfair):** polygraph blips during cold-water and surprise-blast trials — uncontrolled, one-off demonstrations staged for cameras; they enter the file as **narrative**, not evidence.

## 3. Three-Level Excavation

### Level 1 — Observation

What is observed: (a) across a 40+ year ganzfeld archive a small deviation yields effects between 0.013 and 0.13 depending on database rules; (b) the field's "more isolation, more effect" model was not confirmed over the last decade; (c) Bem's open-material effect fell to zero under high-powered replication; (d) dream telepathy's significant results contained blindness failures and did not appear in independent laboratories; (e) twin "telepathy" report rates are high but controlled performance is at chance; (f) MZ twins reared apart show similarity explained by genetics.

### Level 2 — Pattern

The pattern is this: **the effect behaves not like a "pool" but like a "tuned receiver".** The pool model predicts a stable, additive effect independent of where the signal comes from. The tuned-receiver model predicts an effect tied to **pair, state and experimenter** terms — and that is exactly what is observed: the effect varies with laboratory, protocol, the pair's relationship and the novelty of the era; sometimes it even appears as a **reverse deviation** (statistically significant *negative* scores have been reported in psi research, which by itself forces any "hidden signal" model into odd territory).

Second pattern: **the most reliably measured outcome of the effect is its effect on science itself.** The publication of Bem 2011 was the first link in the chain that spread pre-registration, open data and replication standards through social psychology: the lesson "a field that cannot refute its own finding cannot prove it either" was applied to all of psychology outside psi.

### Level 3 — Why is it like this?

Why does this archive neither die nor revive? Because **both camps need the same thing, and its cost cannot be shared:** a decisive answer requires a pre-registered, high-powered, multi-centre experiment run **under the supervision of both camps**. The day such an experiment is announced, both narratives suffer: the positive camp must stop saying "it is definitely there", the negative camp must stop saying "it is definitely absent". The institutional incentive structure does not produce this experiment.

Third layer: the ganzfeld protocol's lifespan is a textbook case of the **decline effect** — new protocols show larger effects in their first years, which then shrink. There are two readings: (a) the effect depends on novelty/curiosity (tuned receiver); (b) the effect is the product of exploratory analysis plus publication bias and melts as the data grow (not a pool but a leak). **The two readings cannot be separated with today's data** — and the only instrument that can separate them is pre-registered replication.

## 4. HIDDEN PATTERN CANDIDATE: HZ-27 — Tuned-Receiver Interaction

> **Candidate Law (HZ-27).** In ganzfeld/dream-type anomalous-perception archives, the observed effect size is not a constant such as "pool strength"; it is the interaction of three terms: **pair bond (matching), state (isolation/sleep) and experimenter/laboratory term** — minus publication bias. The model predicts that **between-laboratory variance** in effect size should exceed sampling error.

**d_observed ≈ (K_pair × S_state × L_experimenter) − B_bias**

**Three predictions:**
1. **The laboratory term:** the same protocol should produce systematically different effects across laboratories, and the differences should not be explainable by sampling error (testable with multi-centre pre-registered data).
2. **The pre-registration rule:** in pre-registered, replication-focused studies the effect should be smaller than in exploratory/novel-protocol studies; registered reports' effect estimates should skew **below** those of non-pre-registered meta-analyses.
3. **The agent test:** if the effect persists when the "sender" is not a human (the target chosen only by a machine, with nobody looking at it), the "mind-to-mind" frame falls and the effect reverts to a decision-structure artefact.

**Discriminating test (HZ-27/1 — adversarial-collaboration protocol, pre-registered).** At least four laboratories, a shared pre-registered protocol; in each laboratory, **both a positive-expectation and a negative-expectation experimenter** are running sessions; ≥ 2,000 trials in total; primary output: pooled effect and the **between-laboratory variance component**; the analysis plan is frozen before the data. Prediction: if the tuned-receiver model is right, the laboratory term will be prominent; if the publication-bias model is right, the effect will shrink once recorded.

**Second test (HZ-27/2 — agentless control arm).** An arm is added to ganzfeld and dream protocols where "the machine chose the target but there is no human sender"; prediction: if the effect appears only in the human-sender arm, the "mind-to-mind" term is real; if it appears in both arms, the "between persons" term falls.

**Third test (HZ-27/3 — the ledger test).** Published original protocols are replicated **verbatim** (the materials are already open) and pre-registered effect estimates are compared with published effects. Prediction: in verbatim replications the effect cannot exceed half of the original (decline/bias signature) — the archive's hardest exam.

## 5. Evidence Thermometer: 🟡 / 🟠 / 🔴

**Why 🟡?** The archive deviation spans decades, has been reported positively in more than one independent meta-analysis, and cannot at first sight be reduced to an ordinary bias story — so saying "it is nothing" would be laziness.

**Why 🟠?** Prospective evidence is weak: Bem's effect could not be replicated with open materials (N = 3,289, d = 0.04); independent dream-laboratory replications are negative; twin performance is at chance. The gulf between archive and prospective evidence shifts the source of the deviation towards archive methods.

**Why 🔴?** There is no mechanism: carrier undefined, no distance/power law, no dose-response relation. This means the evidence cannot be read in favour of a "hidden channel" — but it does not by itself refute **the method-side existence of the effect.**

> **What remains if it is refuted?** If HZ-27/1 and /3 zero the effect under registration, what remains is: (1) the **meta-scientific legacy** — the psi archive is history's most instructive database on publication bias, the decline effect and the value of pre-registration; (2) **sleep and dream science** — the Maimonides line's lasting contribution is the REM-awakening technique and dream-report methodology; (3) **twin research** — behavioural genetics' power to explain human similarity. In the other direction: if the adversarial-collaboration protocol comes back positive, psi history acquires its first "pre-registered multi-centre positive" record, and the program's most contested pillar stands up.

## 6. Practical Implication and Gateway Bridge

1. **The honest promise:** no app can promise that training will establish telepathy; this file's evidence does not permit it. What can be promised is **the receiver state itself:** reducing sensory noise, deepening inner attention, developing dream awareness — each of which produces **measured benefits on its own** (attention, sleep quality, stress).
2. **Application bridge:** Gateway's "distant interaction" narratives should be presented in the app as **personal-state pedagogy**; claims of the "send a message to your partner" kind carry no evidence and are ethically problematic (the other person's consent).
3. **Ethical warning:** "distant healing/influence" practices create a hidden-consent problem and cannot substitute for clinical treatment.
4. **Clinical reality:** sleep hygiene, attention training and dream journalling — this is the **only evidence-backed practical set** in this file's territory.
5. **Research note:** this file connects with UA-07 (the source of the window claim), UA-28 (the same statistical generation), UA-26 (**the natural counterpart of the agentless control arm**) and HZ-30. In the Φ-2 synthesis, HZ-27's "laboratory term" and UA-28's "experimenter–field dependence" will be tested as shared measures.
