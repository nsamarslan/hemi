# UA-18 — Rhythmic Driving: From Drum to Sama, From Individual to Flock

This dossier examines the claim that "rhythm locks not the individual but the herd onto a single oscillator": it separates the folk idea that drumbeats drive the EEG from the physiological synchrony measured at group level. The conclusion is two-tiered: **auditory-motor entrainment is real** (and clinically valuable), **but the claim that drumming directly produces EEG theta failed its best test**; what locks a group is not a cortical oscillation but **shared movement, shared attention and social arousal**.

## 1. Intersection Map: Ethnomusicology, EEG Entrainment, Social Neuroscience and Collective Physiology Look at the Same Drum

Four lines intersect here:

1. **Ethnomusicology** — trance rituals, drumming, sema and other vestibular rituals.
2. **EEG entrainment** — auditory driving, frequency following, drum-tempo claims.
3. **Social neuroscience** — joint movement, synchrony and cooperation.
4. **Collective physiology** — heart and respiration synchrony in groups.

Why do they not converge? Because converging produces a sentence that upsets both sides: **a drum does not inject theta into the brain — but a group under a drum really does synchronize; and the site of that synchrony is the body, not the brainwave.**

## 2. Anomaly Inventory (With Slugs)

### 2.1 Neher's beginning and its limits

Andrew Neher ran the first serious experiment comparing patterns in trance rituals with rhythmic-stimulus effects in the lab: **Neher (1961, *EEG and Clinical Neurophysiology* 13:449–451)** and **Neher (1962, *Human Biology* 34:151–160)**. Ten participants received sequences at 3, 4, 6 and 8 beats per second; "auditory driving" appeared in all participants; the main area was auditory cortex, with additional involvement of occipital regions. Neher likened it to photic driving.

The limits were written up by later critics: (1) Neher read the EEG qualitatively; there was no statistical measure; (2) the laboratory stimulus (constant intensity, constant timbre) does not resemble a real drum (variable, noisy, shaking the body); (3) Rouget's famous objection: "if Neher were right, half of Africa would be in trance all year round" — because entering trance in ritual is a `cultural technique`, not merely a stimulus result.

### 2.2 The fall of "drumming produces theta"

Maxfield and some followers reported that drumming at 4–4.5 beats per second increased the EEG theta component. An independent attempt did **not** confirm this: a master's-level study (2016) using a similar drum pattern found **no significant theta increase and observed decreased alpha activity**; further, a "trance suggestion" manipulation produced no differences in EEG measures. This is the dossier's most important honesty record: **the claim's direct test did not support the claim.**

Note: a single unsuccessful replication at thesis level is not a definitive refutation; but the current picture places the sentence "drumming drives theta" in the **folk science** category.

### 2.3 The solid line: auditory-motor entrainment and beat perception

The solid part of the rhythm literature rests not on EEG theta driving but on **auditory-motor coupling**:

- Nozaradan, Peretz, Missal & Mouraux (2011, *Journal of Neuroscience* 31:10234–10240) "tagged" separate neural responses at **beat and meter frequencies** while people listened to music (steady-state evoked potentials). The brain **reproduces the beat internally** — a solid finding that explains why moving to music feels so natural.
- Large and Snyder (2009, *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* 1169:46–57) formulated beat perception as "neural resonance".
- Groove studies (Janata, Tomic & Haberman, 2012, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 141:54–75; Witek et al., 2014, *PLoS ONE* 9:e94446) showed that syncopated rhythms produce movement and pleasure.
- Clinical line: rhythmic auditory stimulation (RAS) markedly improves gait in Parkinson's disease (one of the strongest clinical applications of music therapy; work by Thaut and colleagues).

This goes in the "mechanism is real" box: rhythm couples with the motor system in a **well-documented** way. But that does not mean the drum "is a brainwave" — the motor system's timer is not a cortical band.

### 2.4 Collective synchrony: measured and real

- Konvalinka et al. (2011, *PNAS* 108:8514–8519) measured **heart-rate synchronization between performers and spectators** in a fire-walking ritual; synchrony was modulated by the spectator's relationship to the performer.
- Wiltermuth and Heath (2009, *Psychological Science* 20:1–5) showed that synchronous movement increases **cooperation**.
- Reddish, Fischer and Bulbulia (2013, *PLoS ONE* 8:e71182) found that collective synchrony strengthened perceived solidarity at a celebration in New Zealand.
- The joint-action literature (Sebanz, Bekkering & Knoblich, 2006, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 10:70–76) shows that action planning merges in social contexts.

Regarding sema specifically, the honest state: there is **no** controlled EEG/simultaneous physiology study; sema's strongest physiological component is the **vestibular stimulation** of spinning, whose effects on affect and state belong to a separate literature (connected to UA-32 and HZ-39). The sentence "sema locks the collective through cortical synchrony" is an inference, not data.

## 3. Three-Level Excavation

### Level 1 — Observation

What is observed: (a) auditory-motor driving and beat perception are solidly demonstrated (SS-EPs, groove, RAS); (b) the claim "drumming drives EEG theta" was **not supported** in its best test; (c) group-level heart-rate synchrony has been measured (fire ritual); (d) synchronous movement increases cooperation and solidarity; (e) entering trance depends on **cultural technique and context**, not only on the stimulus.

### Level 2 — Pattern

The pattern is this: **the lock must be sought on two levels, and only one of them is real.**

- **First level (real): the motor-attention level.** The drum is a metronome for *movement*; motor timing built on beat perception places the individual (walking, dancing, spinning in sema) on a shared time base.
- **Second level (unsupported): the cortical EEG level.** The claim that drumming directly drives a band failed its direct test.

So the sentence "the herd locks onto a single oscillator" can be true if **the oscillator is the shared external timekeeper, not the internal clock**: the group locks onto the same tempo at the level *behaviour*; physiological synchrony (heart, respiration) is its indirect trace.

A second pattern: **the function of rhythm in ritual is not making "trance" but making "belonging."** The output of synchrony is social glue more than altered consciousness (Wiltermuth & Heath; Reddish).

### Level 3 — Why is it so?

Why can a herd share external timing rather than individual internal oscillations? Because two separate brains cannot match their internal rhythms (alpha, theta) — those are individual and hidden. But **movement and attention are public**: both are visible, measurable and matchable. So cultures built the herd lock through the most **accessible** channel: the body.

Third layer: **ritual is "public key" sharing among nervous systems.** You cannot share the brain's inner state; you can share the body. Drum, sema and chant are the **coarse synchronization protocol** of that sharing — not a cortical frequency recipe.

## 4. HIDDEN PATTERN CANDIDATE: HZ-18 — Collective Driving Protocol (corrected)

> **Candidate Law (HZ-18, corrected).** Rhythm locks the group, not the individual; but the lock is built not in a cortical EEG band but in the product of **shared movement timing × shared attention × social arousal**. A drum is not a "theta injector" but a **metronome among bodies**; group-level heart/respiration synchrony is the indirect trace of that lock.

Simplified statement:

**S_herd ≈ M_shared × A_shared × U_social** &nbsp;&nbsp; and &nbsp;&nbsp; **∂(EEG theta)/∂(drum tempo) ≈ 0** (in direct tests)

where M is shared movement matching, A shared attention, U social arousal.

**Three predictions:**
1. **Movement follows, EEG does not:** In group drumming, motor and cardiac synchrony should track the drum tempo; EEG theta power should not track the drum frequency.
2. **The social condition is necessary:** Listening to the same rhythm alone should not create synchrony; **moving together** should. The key to the lock is partnership, not rhythm.
3. **The output is social:** The herd lock should measurably raise cooperation/solidarity outcomes; "altered consciousness" outcomes, meanwhile, should depend more on expectation and context than on the stimulus.

**Discriminating test (HZ-18/1 — tempo and channel).** Three arms: 4.5 Hz drum, 8 Hz drum and irregular (asynchronous) percussion; per participant: EEG + chest band (ECG/respiration) + wrist accelerometer. Prediction: motor/cardiac synchrony should be equally **high** in the 4.5 Hz and 8 Hz arms (because the carrier of the lock is movement), while EEG theta should be **unrelated** to tempo. Result: Neher-style "theta driving" is not supported.

**Second test (HZ-18/2 — partnership manipulation).** Participants hear the same drumming (a) alone, (b) with a partner, (c) with a virtual partner (on screen). Prediction: interpersonal physiological synchrony forms only in (b) and (c), not in (a) — rhythm is necessary but not sufficient.

**Third test (HZ-18/3 — field, preregistered).** In a ritual community (sema, chant, drum circle), pre/post measures: physiological synchrony, belonging/cooperation scales, subjective state. Prediction: the synchrony ↔ belonging relation is strong; the synchrony ↔ EEG band change relation is weak.

## 5. Evidence Thermometer: 🟢 / 🟡 / 🟠

**Why 🟢 (entrainment and social synchrony)?** Neural responses at beat/meter tagged with SS-EPs, groove studies, RAS's clinical effect, heart-rate synchrony measurements and the synchrony-cooperation link — all four replicated by independent teams.

**Why 🟡 (ritual literature)?** Field studies are scattered; "sema/trance EEG" barely exists; samples are small and expectation effects uncontrolled.

**Why 🟠 (the drum-theta claim)?** Not supported in its direct test; laboratory paradigms do not represent the body-shaking, social, contextual nature of ritual.

> **What would remain if falsified?** If HZ-18/1 shows EEG theta really does track tempo, this dossier's "no cortical driving" sentence falls and the Neher line revives. In the opposite direction: if HZ-18/2 finds synchrony even in the alone condition, the lock turns out to be built by rhythm rather than partnership. In either case, the **clinical and social outputs** (RAS, synchrony-cooperation) stand independently of the programme.

## 6. Practical Takeaway and Gateway Bridge

1. **The honest promise:** Moving to rhythm (walking, dance, sema, group work) produces **reliable** effects on attention, mood and motor function; the mechanism is auditory-motor coupling and social synchrony, not cortical theta driving.
2. **Sessions and expected benefit:** The app's rhythmic sessions give their strongest effect when combined with movement; a passive-listening path amounts to voice guidance and attention practice — not "brainwave programming."
3. **Group practice.** The dossier's most practical recommendation: the power of rituals comes largely from **doing them together**; if the app supports this in a social context, it builds an evidence-consistent foundation.
4. **Caution.** Deep-trance claims and practices like prolonged fast spinning (vestibular load, fall risk) require clinical and safety evaluation; in epilepsy, vertigo and cardiac conditions, rhythmic stimulation should be cleared with a physician first.
5. **Research note:** This dossier connects with UA-01 (sound protocols), UA-19 (space acoustics), UA-32 (flow) and HZ-06 (harmonic isolation); the Φ-2 synthesis will test the "collective driving" counterpart at the level of HZ-27 (Ganzfeld) and social networks.
