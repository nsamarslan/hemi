# UA-04 — Extracerebral Distributed Memory: Where in the Body Are Memories Stored?

This dossier examines the claim that "memory is not in the brain but distributed across the whole body": fear carried across generations by sperm RNA, a decapitated planarian remembering its maze, preference changes after heart transplants. The conclusion is threefold: (1) **"memory" at the cellular level (behaviour change according to a temporal pattern) exists in every cell** and was shown directly in human cell lines in 2024; (2) **the immune system and tissues are genuine distributed memory systems**; (3) **the claim that personal/episodic memories are stored outside the brain is unsupported** — planarian and transplant narratives cannot fill that gap.

## 1. Intersection Map: Molecular Epigenetics, Regeneration Biology, Transplantation and Basal Cognition Stare at the Same Question

Four lines intersect here:

1. **Molecular epigenetics** — DNA methylation, histone modifications, small RNAs: the chemistry of "cellular memory."
2. **Regeneration biology** — planaria, hydra, amphibian models: what information survives while tissue rebuilds?
3. **Transplantation** — organ grafts and "cellular memory" narratives; chimerism; graft-host signalling.
4. **Basal cognition** — learning in organisms without a nervous system: single cells, fungi, plants.

Why do they not converge? Because converging produces a sentence that unsettles both sides: **cells really do "remember" — but cellular memory and personal memory are different things; showing one does not prove the other.**

## 2. Anomaly Inventory (With Slugs)

### 2.1 Kukushkin 2024: human cells without a nervous system "learn"

**Kukushkin, Carney, Tabassum and Carew (2024, *Nature Communications* 15:9635)** used two different non-neural human cell lines (kidney- and nerve-tissue derived) with a short-lived luciferase reporter driven by a CREB-dependent promoter. Finding: **four spaced pulses of stimulation produce stronger and longer-lasting "memory" expression than one "massed" pulse**; the effect depends on ERK and CREB activation, and inhibiting either blocks it. The authors' statement is clear: "canonical features of memory do not necessarily depend on neural circuitry; they can be embedded in the dynamics of signalling cascades conserved across different cell types."

This is the dossier's 🟢 core — with its boundary: what is measured is **recognition and retention of a temporal pattern**, not a personal memory.

### 2.2 Planaria: the decapitated worm and a contested memory

Planarians (flatworms) regrow severed heads. The classic claim is **McConnell's (1962) "memory transfer through cannibalism"** experiments — considered methodologically unreliable today (uncontrolled, poorly replicable).

The modern, more serious line: **Shomrat and Levin (2013, *Journal of Experimental Biology* 216:3799–3810)** reported, with an automated training paradigm, that long-term memory in planarians **persisted after the head was cut off and regrown**. This is intriguing, but two serious limits apply:

1. When planarians are decapitated, **ventral nerve cords and a nerve net remain in the body** — so "no brain" does not mean "no neural tissue."
2. The finding comes from a single laboratory line and lacks independent, blind, preregistered replication.

So: "Memory in this system is not confined to the head." Can be said. "Memory is outside the brain." Cannot.

### 2.3 Fear through sperm RNA: transgenerational epigenetic transmission

**Dias and Ressler (2014, *Nature Neuroscience* 17:89–96)**: offspring of mice conditioned to fear an odour were more sensitive to the same odour; methylation changes were reported at the relevant olfactory receptor gene in the first generation. **Gapp et al. (2014)**: microRNA signatures were reported in the male offspring of trauma-model mice.

The honest record: this field is **contested**. There are replication difficulties, small effect sizes, protocol differences, and failed attempts by some independent groups. Yet it is not entirely "collapsed": experimental lines showing that small RNA can carry a behavioural signature via sperm, and mechanistic proposals, continue. In this dossier it sits at 🟡: **"possible; it has not become a demonstrated strong law of nature."**

### 2.4 Trained immunity: distributed memory, demonstrated

Innate immune cells also "remember": **Netea et al. (2016, *Science* 352:aaf1098)** systematized the concept of "trained immunity"; BCG vaccination was shown to epigenetically reprogram monocytes toward stronger responses (Kleinnijenhuis et al., 2012, *PNAS*). So the body already has a measurable **distributed memory system** working independently of the brain — and this is the scientific backbone of the modern "cellular memory" claims.

### 2.5 Preference changes after transplant: the narrative layer

Retrospective survey studies by Pearsall and colleagues in the 1990s–2000s (dozens of cases) reported changes in heart transplant recipients resembling the donor's preferences and interests. This dataset is:

1. **retrospective, self-selected** (memorable and tellable cases get recorded),
2. **control-free** (post-transplant life changes, drugs, trauma and redirected attention naturally alter preferences),
3. **not blind** (interpretations made with the donor profile known),
4. and **mechanism-free**.

The scientifically real part of the same line: the heart has a genuine **intrinsic cardiac nervous system** (tens of thousands of neurons); donor-derived cells and microchimerism can persist in the recipient for years. So "donor tissue lives on in the recipient" is true; "the donor's memories pass to the recipient" has not been shown.

### 2.6 The wider channel: basal cognition evidence

- **Physarum** (multicellular but brainless slime mould): Boisseau, Vogel and Dussutour (2016, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 283:20160446) demonstrated habituation learning — response to a repeated harmless stimulus decreases, and the habituation transfers to other conditions.
- **Plant priming**: defence responses accelerate after repeated stress — a memory-like cellular phenomenon.
- **Single-cell learning**: "memory" in bacterial chemotaxis (via methylation state), and phenotypic memory in persister cells.

All of this shows that **memory is an evolutionarily far older property than the nervous system**.

## 3. Three-Level Excavation

### Level 1 — Observation

What is observed: (a) non-neural human cells "remember" a spaced-stimulus pattern (Kukushkin 2024); (b) trained immunity produces brain-independent distributed memory (Netea 2016); (c) brainless organisms such as Physarum habituate (Boisseau 2016); (d) planarians can retain training after head regeneration — one lab, with the body nerve net as a confound (Shomrat & Levin 2013); (e) transgenerational fear transmission was reported but replication is contested (Dias & Ressler 2014); (f) transplant narratives are retrospective and uncontrolled (the Pearsall line).

### Level 2 — Pattern

The pattern is this: **"memory" is not one thing but a hierarchy.**

- **Level A — cell state:** a persistent molecular state tuned by a temporal pattern (methylation, kinase activity, RNA signature). Present in every cell. Real and measured.
- **Level B — tissue/organism systems:** immunity, tissue repair, habituation. Distributed, real.
- **Level C — personal/episodic memory:** an indexed, recallable record producing subjective experience. So far demonstrated **only in the central nervous system**.

The confusion arises when A and B evidence is read as producing C.

A second pattern: **the brain is not the storage layer but the access layer.** The body keeps "state codes" everywhere; the brain's job is to integrate those codes into addressable memory. Why? Because access requires **indexing** over many distributed states — and indexing is not cheap. The nervous system is the organ evolution built to provide that indexing service.

### Level 3 — Why is it so?

Why did memory predate the nervous system, and why does it still sit in every cell? Because **every cell must act according to its past**: nutrient patterns, toxin patterns, wound patterns. Cellular state therefore inevitably carries "a faint memory."

Third layer: **personal memory is a special indexing of cellular memory.** The brain works not by forbidding cellular memory but by making it *addressable*. This is why brain damage does not erase cellular memory — it breaks access; and why the sentence "the body remembers" is literally true but psychologically misplaced.

## 4. HIDDEN PATTERN CANDIDATE: HZ-04 — Distributed Memory and the Access Key

> **Candidate Law (HZ-04, corrected).** Memory lives not in a single store but in a **distributed repertoire of cell- and tissue-state codes**. The brain's contribution is the **indexing and addressability** of those codes (the access key). Cellular memory exists in every cell and has been demonstrated; the extracerebral storage of personal memory has not.

Simplified statement:

**Memory_accessible ≈ E(I) × Σ_c D_c** &nbsp;&nbsp; where D_c = cell state codes, E = access/indexing function (nervous system), I = identity/integration.

**Three predictions:**
1. **Spaced learning everywhere:** the massed-spaced signature should appear in non-neural cell lines too (shown by Kukushkin; prediction: also in primary cells and across species).
2. **The planarian test:** training retained after head regeneration should disappear if the body nerve net is pharmacologically silenced — meaning memory may be carried not by "the body" but by the **remaining neural tissue**.
3. **The transplant test:** whatever can transfer via donor tissue should be limited to measurable cellular/molecular signatures (chimerism, donor cells, inflammatory profile); "preference transfer" should **not** appear under blind, prospective, objective measures.

**Discriminating test (HZ-04/1 — replication and human primary cells).** The Kukushkin paradigm is replicated by independent laboratories under a preregistered protocol in primary human cells (e.g., fibroblasts, hepatocytes); prediction: the effect emerges robustly. This strengthens "memory = a fundamental cellular property."

**Second test (HZ-04/2 — planaria, dissociating).** Three arms: (a) decapitation only, (b) decapitation + body nerve-net ablation, (c) sham. Prediction: training residue disappears in (b) — refuting the "body memory store" claim and supporting the "widely distributed neural net" model.

**Third test (HZ-04/3 — prospective transplant cohort).** Heart transplant recipients are followed in a preregistered prospective cohort (donor and recipient profiles blinded) with objective measures (preference tests, taste/food preferences, music liking), not questionnaires. Prediction: no meaningful transfer is **seen**; observed changes are explained by life events, drugs and expectation.

## 5. Evidence Thermometer: 🟢 / 🟡 / 🟠 / 🔴

**Why 🟢 (cellular memory and immunity)?** Kukushkin 2024, trained immunity (Netea 2016; Kleinnijenhuis 2012) and Physarum habituation (Boisseau 2016) — directly measured, supported by independent lines.

**Why 🟡 (transgenerational)?** The Dias & Ressler and Gapp lines are important but contested on replication, sample and protocol; the evidence sits at "strong hypothesis" level.

**Why 🟠 (planaria)?** Single laboratory, the body neural tissue confound, and no independent replication; the classic McConnell experiments are considered unreliable.

**Why 🔴 (memory after transplant)?** Retrospective, uncontrolled, non-blind survey data; no mechanism; no prospective evidence.

> **What would remain if falsified?** If HZ-04/2 fails to erase the training residue with body nerve-net ablation, the "distributed body store" claim earns its first serious support and HZ-04's "access key" formulation needs revision. In the opposite direction: if the Kukushkin finding collapses under independent replication, the cellular memory claim weakens — but trained immunity and habituation learning stand independently. The transplant narratives carry no scientific weight anyway.

## 6. Practical Takeaway and Gateway Bridge

1. **The honest promise:** your body "remembers" — but that is cells tuning their states to the past; the stage of your personal memories is still your brain. Separating the two makes the practice not mystical but **correct**.
2. **The value of spaced repetition:** the Kukushkin finding reminds us that spaced learning is a rule coded *at every cellular level* — practically relevant for exercise, sleep scheduling and learning design.
3. **Immune bridge:** "trained immunity" (vaccination, movement, sleep) is directly connected to the body's distributed memory; the app's health layer can rest on this channel.
4. **Caution.** This dossier supports no "body memory therapy" claim; trauma treatment and memory processing are evidence-based clinical fields (e.g., EMDR, trauma-focused cognitive therapy). Frames such as "unprocessed emotion is stored in the body" can be useful as metaphor; they are **not defensible as a mechanism law**.
5. **Research note:** this dossier shares the "cell leaving/staying in the network" theme with UA-16 (engram), UA-39 (self-model) and HZ-09 (onco-bioelectric alienation); the Φ-2 synthesis will test the commonality of the "access key" idea with HZ-03 (receiver-locked coupling).
