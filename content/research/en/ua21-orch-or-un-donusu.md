# UA-21 — The Return of Orch-OR: Do Anesthetics Bind to Microtubules?

**Short answer:** Microtubules (MTs) are genuine molecular structures potentially relevant to anesthetic action; 2024 rat and 2026 mouse experiments found that MT stabilization delayed the loss of righting reflex under isoflurane. This **does not confirm Orch-OR**: behavioral onset of anesthesia, a molecular target, long-lived quantum coherence and quantum reduction as the cause of consciousness are separate claims. The 2026 mouse paper also cites a study in which two-week administration of a similar stabilizer *increased* sensitivity in the opposite direction. The decisive question is not "are there quantum effects?" but **does the anesthetic's consciousness-related effect depend on a measurable quantum optical state independently of classical MT functions?**

## 1. Intersection Map: Pharmacology × Cytoskeleton × Quantum Optics × Consciousness

**Microtubule:** an intracellular tube assembled from tubulin proteins; in neurons, important for axonal transport, cytoskeletal stability, development and synapse maintenance. These functions alone could alter responses to anesthesia by *classical means*. **Anesthetic:** inhaled agents such as isoflurane; action may involve ion channels, synaptic proteins, mitochondria and the cytoskeleton rather than a single receptor. **Quantum optics:** collective properties of light-excited states in aromatic protein residues; optical coherence is not equivalent to a quantum mechanism of behavior. **Orch-OR:** Hameroff and Penrose's account equating "objective reduction" of quantum states in MTs with conscious moments; it requires a physical mechanism and a characteristic timescale.

Place four levels along a chain: **drug binds MT → MT physical state changes → that change causes loss of consciousness → the cause is collapse of an MT's long-lived quantum state.** Each arrow needs separate data. Most errors come from measuring the first two arrows and declaring the fourth proven.

## 2. Anomaly Inventory: Striking Findings and Their Limits

### 2.1 Rats: a large-looking effect, a small design

Khan et al. (2024, *eNeuro*, DOI 10.1523/ENEURO.0291-24.2024) measured **8 male rats** first under control conditions, then following subcutaneous **0.75 mg/kg epothilone B**. Under 4% isoflurane, latency to loss of righting reflex (LORR) grew by **69 seconds** on average; p = **0.0016**, standardized effect d = **1.9**. A further 4 animals were used for tolerance controls. The team argued that the estimated increase from repeated exposure across the control sequence was about **40 seconds** and thus did not fully explain the 69-second change.

**Design debt:** control and drug conditions always followed the same order; blinding was insufficient (explicit in peer-review synthesis); time to regain the righting reflex (RORR) could not be interpreted owing to variability; possible off-target arousal effects were not isolated. LORR is a coarse motor-behavior threshold, not a direct measure of surgical awareness or subjective experience. Nonetheless, the finding is not zero: a pharmacological intervention significantly shifted an isoflurane-related behavior.

### 2.2 Mouse follow-up: repetition, and an opposite signal

Huang et al. (2026, *Neuropharmacology* 287, 110834; DOI 10.1016/j.neuropharm.2026.110834) report that **one 8 mg/kg epothilone B dose** in mice lengthened latency to LORR under isoflurane by about **29 seconds**; within-subject d ≈ **0.8**; small relative to between-mouse variability and diminished in subsequent days. The mouse paper's abstract also cites an earlier study in which **two weeks of a similar stabilizer increased anesthetic sensitivity** in mice. The authors discuss species, dose and compensatory adaptation as possible distinctions.

This **falsifies** the simple rule "MT stabilization always increases anesthetic resistance." A 29-second single-dose behavioral shift strengthens MT's functional relevance; long-term reversal and possible dose/sex effects complicate the causal chain. **Independent teams, randomized order and molecular selectivity** are needed; a follow-up in two species by the same group is valuable replication, but not independent replication.

### 2.3 Tubulin photophysics: quantum optics ≠ quantum consciousness

Babcock et al. (2024, *J. Phys. Chem. B* 128, 4035–4046, DOI 10.1021/acs.jpcb.3c07936) found **increased ultraviolet fluorescence quantum yields** in large tryptophan networks in microtubules; a theoretical model predicts collective **superradiant and subradiant modes** for >10⁵ tryptophan transition dipoles; tubulin assembly changes the optical signature. This physics defeats the easy dismissal that "no quantum optical effect can exist in warm matter."

But the measurements are from **UV-excited protein solutions**: is sufficient UV excitation available in a living neuron, do absorbers such as ferritin matter, is the optical state sufficiently long-lived, and does it affect synaptic decisions? Four separate unanswered questions. **Enhanced optical yield observed at thermal equilibrium** is not behavioral quantum unity lasting hundreds of milliseconds in an awake brain. The measured causal efficacy of UA-11's classical ephaptic field and the proposed role of quantum protein radiation must likewise be kept separate.

### 2.4 The 2013 line and its antagonists

In 2013, with anthracene-based general anesthetics, MT stabilizers increased the tadpole immobilization dose (*JACS*, DOI 10.1021/ja311171u); similar effects were reported for some neurosteroids. This adds to the evidence that **MTs can be a pharmacological target** beyond isoflurane; it does not mean "MTs are the only target of all anesthetics." No reason exists to abandon receptor and network-disconnection explanations in the multi-target anesthesia literature. Both extreme readings fail: (a) "MT effects are impossible" is wrong; (b) "MT effects establish Orch-OR" is also wrong. Decoherence critiques demand a timescale; a quantum-biology answer must measure the environment, lifetime and behavioral bridge rather than merely assert that "quantum biology is possible."

## 3. Three-Level Excavation

**Level 1 — Observation.** Time to LORR changes after single-dose MT stabilization in two species; in the optical laboratory MT assembly changes the fluorescence result. Clinical awareness assessment, emergence dynamics and simultaneous optical measurements in living neurons have not yet been bundled with those behavioral experiments.

**Level 2 — Pattern.** The "anesthesia node" may be part of a multi-target network involving MTs: the cytoskeleton alters synaptic-receptor location, axonal transport, cell energy and firing thresholds. A quantum state remains possible, but we lack a test of **causal mediation**. The opposite direction after long-term mouse treatment illustrates the danger of reading a one-way "stabilization → more consciousness" law into the data.

**Level 3 — Why this pattern?** The historical flow turns each new measurement into a launchpad for an older speculation: Penrose–Hameroff in the 1990s → rejection via decoherence in the warm brain → optical quantum-biology experiments → behavioral result in 2024 → "quantum consciousness proven" headlines. The first task, however, is to **place new results in competition with classical explanations**. Paradigm change is neither forbidden nor cheap: all four links must survive.

## 4. HIDDEN PATTERN CANDIDATE: HZ-21 — Quantum Skeleton, a Mediation Test

> **Candidate law:** The MT component of anesthetic sensitivity contains two potentially separable channels: the **classical physiological functions** of the cytoskeleton and possible **quantum optical mediation**. The second term exists only if selective disruption of the optical state predicts behavior while the first is held fixed.

**ΔLORR ≈ α·ΔM_classical + β·ΔQ_optical + γ·ΔR_receptor + ε**

The coefficients are unknown; **β ≠ 0 cannot be derived from today's data**. The equation is a proposal for partitioning mechanisms, not a proof. If α and β covary, behavior alone cannot separate them.

**Discriminating preregistered experiment:** (1) epothilone B/vehicle against an isoflurane dose–response — randomized, blinded crossover order; both sexes, multiple labs; (2) measure MT assembly and an **excitation-independent or physiologically calibrated** optical signature in parallel; if UV optics is used, independently check that measurement does not disturb the biological medium; (3) try a selective perturbation or restoration of optical signal with synaptic transmission and MT stability held fixed; (4) add EEG spectra, responsiveness and emergence time to LORR; (5) a declared-in-advance mediation analysis. **β is supported only if arm 3 is selective and reproducible.** Such selectivity may not be technically possible yet; that means "currently untested," not "confirmed."

**Failure conditions to prespecify in the proposed study:** the optical signature is absent in living tissue; it exists but does not track behavior; classical MT variables explain the stabilizer effect without the optical signature; emergence and EEG fail to follow LORR; the 69/29-second effect vanishes in independent blinded order-controlled replication. Each condition falsifies a different arrow; a null result alone does not deny the cellular biology of MTs.

## 5. Evidence Thermometer and Falsification Box

**🟢** MTs' cellular biology and the publication of single-dose epothilone B–isoflurane behavioral interactions in two species. Neither directly measures conscious experience.

**🟡** Collective UV optical behavior of tubulin/microtubule networks demonstrated in vitro. Interpretive boundary: fluorescence is not functional coherence in a living brain.

**🟠** MT's specific causal role as an anesthetic target (versus multi-target pharmacology), and mediation by a quantum optical state — no selective intervention for the latter. Both species follow-ups are from one team; the opposite long-term result limits generalization. This slot's overall thermometer stays 🟠.

**🔴** It has not been demonstrated that Penrose objective reduction creates a mental moment or that isoflurane switches it off.

> **If falsified, what remains?** If the epothilone interaction vanishes under independent blinding, the **essential cellular functions of MTs and optical properties of isolated protein** remain. If the interaction repeats but optical mediation is null, an **MT target** (a classical route) survives while Orch-OR's special link weakens. Even strong optical mediation would not, by itself, prove Penrose reduction or an identity with phenomenal consciousness. One successful intermediate arrow is not all four.

## 6. Practical Implications and the Gateway Bridge

Anesthesia is a **clinician-supervised procedure**; this dossier offers no self-experiment, and MT stabilizers or anesthetic substances should never be used for meditation or focus. Gateway audio rhythms and bodily experiences are not measurements of MT quantum states. Similar language to UA-11's field-integrity pattern is acceptable, but a **classical ephaptic field** and a **UV quantum-optical protein state** are not the same mechanism. As with UA-23's interfacial water, the bridge that prevents the jump from "measured at an interface" to "carries consciousness throughout the body" is the **mediation experiment**.

Honest formulation for the reader: "MTs may play a role in anesthesia" 🟠; "MTs have quantum optical properties" 🟡 (laboratory); "consciousness is Orch-OR" 🔴. Neither premature victory nor reflex ridicule. The app's clinical safety messages and independence note still apply.
