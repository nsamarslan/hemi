# UA-36 — Interoceptive Depth: What Does Counting Heartbeats Measure?

This dossier examines why the sense of the body's interior is the **main channel** underlying emotions, decisions and the sense of self. The fact that the measurement method changes the result is the field's most honestly recorded weakness; yet interoception's regulatory function stands independently. We close with a candidate law proposing that interoception is not "a feeling" but a **signal of significance assignment**.

## 1. Intersection Map: Do Neuroanatomy, Emotion Theory and Clinical Measurement Measure the Same Thing?

Four lines intersect here:

1. **Neuroanatomy** — the insular cortex and the cortical map of interoception.
2. **Emotion theory** — the view that emotion is an interpretation of body state.
3. **Decision neuroscience** — interoception's contribution to decision-making.
4. **Clinical measurement** — the heartbeat-counting task and its derivatives.

Why do they not merge? Because merging them produces a sentence that shakes both psychometrics and theory: **interoception may be the common variable beneath emotion and decision; but the measurement tool changes that variable.** There is a **measurement-effect coupling** in this field, and it is its most serious methodological constraint.

## 2. Anomaly Inventory (with citations)

### 2.1 The anatomical basis: the cortical map of interoception

Craig (2002, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 3:655–666; 2009, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 10:59–70) showed interoception is not merely "inner-organ sense" but an **engineered representation** of body state: the lamina I spinothalamic pathway reaches the posterior insula and passes to the anterior insula, building the map of subjective "body feeling". In Craig's own phrase, it is "the screen on which the body's thermostat is read". This map does not show **who you are**, but **what state you are in right now**.

### 2.2 Measurement: the heartbeat-counting task and three dimensions

The heartbeat-counting task developed by Schandry (1981) asks participants to count their own heartbeats and compares the reported count with the actual one. Garfinkel et al. (2015, *Biological Psychology* 104:65–74; 2016) showed interoception contains three distinct dimensions:

- **Accuracy:** objectively, how well do you sense?
- **Sensibility:** how much do you trust your own perception?
- **Awareness:** the correspondence between accuracy and confidence.

This distinction matters: high confidence and high accuracy are not the same thing, and their **mismatches** are diagnostic in psychopathology.

### 2.3 The weakness of measurement: floor effects and bias

Zamariola et al. (2018, *Biological Psychology* 139:47–54) documented the task's core weaknesses: a large share of participants give **confidently wrong** answers; the task is open to bias (strategic guessing, prior knowledge, assumptions about the scoring interval). Desmedt et al. (2022, *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*) and many laboratories accept that the task is a noisy proxy rather than a measure of a stable trait.

**The honest conclusion:** the measurement-error rate is high in this field; basing a result on "interoceptive measurement" is therefore weak evidential ground. But that does not erase the **functional relations**: even a noisy measure carries information if it correlates with outcomes.

### 2.4 Emotion: the interpretation of body state

Barrett (2017, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 21:807–827; and her constructed-emotion theory) argues that emotion is the **conceptually labelled** version of body state: interoception provides a noisy signal; cultural and individual concepts interpret it as "fear", "excitement" or "hunger". Pollatos et al. (2005, *Cognitive Brain Research* 25:948–962) and Füstös et al. (2013, *Frontiers in Psychology* 4:199) showed that higher interoceptive accuracy is associated with greater emotion-regulation capacity.

### 2.5 Decision: the body signal under uncertainty

Damasio's somatic-marker hypothesis proposes that decisions are guided by body signals. Werner et al. (2009, *Psychophysiology* 46:1123–1129) showed interoceptive accuracy predicting performance in intuitive/ambiguous decisions: better interoception → more consistent decisions under uncertainty. Dunn et al. (2010, *Psychological Science* 21:1835–1844) reviewed the literature and documented that interoception serves as a shared "body input" for appetite, emotion and decision-making.

### 2.6 The clinic: anxiety, panic and a "false alarm"

Paulus and Stein (2006, *Biological Psychiatry* 60:383–387) proposed modelling anxiety as **over-weighting** of interoception: when the body signal is misread or gains excessive confidence, panic attacks appear as "false alarms". Domschke et al. (2010, *Behavioral and Brain Functions* 6:39) documented interoceptive deviations in panic disorder. The reviews by Khalsa et al. (2018, *Biological Psychiatry: CNNI* 3:501–513) show the relationship is **bidirectional**: some disorders involve hypersensitive interoception, others (depersonalization, alexithymia) involve blunted interoception.

### 2.7 Training: does regular practice change interoception?

Fischer, Messner and Pollatos (2017, *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 11:452 — an 8-week body-scan intervention) and Bornemann et al. (2015, *Frontiers in Psychology* 5:1504) showed increased interoceptive accuracy in regular meditators. Despite measurement weaknesses, the **direction is consistent**: any practice turning attention inward increases the processing resources allocated to the body signal — producing a measurable gain.

### 2.8 Between interoception and exteroception: body or world?

A final anomaly: traditional meditation literature reports that "turning inward" **weakens** outer perception. The laboratory documents the opposite as well: interoception and exteroception are not in pure competition; there is a bilateral "awareness flexibility". This links directly to UA-05's filter curve: the lifespan filter determines which channel dominates.

## 3. Three-Level Excavation

### Level 1 — Observation
- The cortical map of interoception produces a representation of body state (Craig).
- Interoception splits into three dimensions: accuracy, sensibility, awareness (Garfinkel).
- The heartbeat-counting task is open to measurement error (Zamariola 2018).
- Interoceptive accuracy relates to emotion regulation and decision under uncertainty.
- Anxiety is over-weighting of interoception; alexithymia is under-weighting.
- Regular contemplative practice raises interoception.

### Level 2 — Pattern
A two-component pattern: **(A) The main channel.** Interoception is not "an emotion" but a signal source that **assigns significance** to the decision and emotion systems. When the brain senses a body state, it does not merely produce a feeling; it assigns that feeling **source and priority.** **(B) Measurement-effect coupling.** The measurement tool (attention, counting, task) changes the signal itself: measuring a signal becomes part of producing it. This is a structural constraint on interoception research — and also a clue: interoception is a channel that **opens when interrogated**.

### Level 3 — Why is it designed this way?
Because an organism producing a decision or emotion needs not only "information from the outer world" but also "information from its own state". But this second source lacks the objective reference the outer world has: the heartbeat is real, yet its perception is open to interpretation. The system therefore builds a two-layer solution:

1. **Raw inner signal** (cardiac, respiratory, enteric afferents) — flowing continuously.
2. **Source and priority assignment** — when attention labels the signal "important", it is amplified.

Interoception is thus not a raw measurement but a **filtered and weighted** channel. That is why the signal changes with state and attention — and why measurement-effect coupling is built into the architecture.

## 4. HIDDEN PATTERN CANDIDATE: HZ-36 — The Interoceptive Main Channel

> **Candidate Law (HZ-36).** Interoception is a common variable beneath emotion and decision; its function is not "to produce feeling" but to **assign significance and priority** to body state. Signal quality rises with the resources attention directs to it; therefore the measurement tool changes the measured signal.

Simplified expression:

**K_body ≈ I_raw × A_attention** &nbsp;&nbsp; and &nbsp; **Emotion_label = K_body × Concept**

**Three predictions:**
1. **The three dimensions should dissociate:** accuracy, sensibility and awareness should relate to different personality/pathology patterns; collapsing two of them into a single "interoception score" should **reduce** predictive power (a dissociation test).
2. **Channel separation:** different inner-organ channels (cardiac, respiratory, gastric) are **not** equally well sensed in the same person; interoception should be a sum of multiple sub-channels, not a single central "thermometer".
3. **Training effect is channel-specific:** meditation training should strengthen cardiac/respiratory channels while the gastric/enteric channel may not rise at the same rate (because practices direct attention to heart and breath).

**Discriminating test (HZ-36/1 — three channels).** In the same participant, three accuracy tests: heartbeat counting, respiratory awareness (nasal airflow detection), gastric emptying sensation (with simultaneous electrogastrography). Prediction: cross-channel correlations **low to moderate**; "interoception" will be a **profile**, not a single trait.

**Second test (HZ-36/2 — measurement-effect coupling).** The same task is given under two framings: (A) "observe your own body", (B) "an objective performance test". Prediction: reported counts **rise** in arm A (attention amplification) and **fall** in arm B (shift to an external reference); both arms will be biased relative to the true signal. This shows directly that the measurement tool changes the measured thing.

**Third test (HZ-36/3 — the decision bridge).** Relate the interoceptive profile (three channels) to decision performance under uncertainty (Iowa Gambling Task); prediction: the relation will be **channel-specific** — the cardiac channel linking more strongly to emotional decision, the gastric channel to approach-avoidance choice.

## 5. Evidence Thermometer: 🟢 (functional relations) / 🟡 (measurement reliability)

**Why 🟢 (function)?** The insular map, the three-dimension distinction, the emotion-regulation relation, the decision contribution and the training effect were shown in multiple independent studies.

**Why 🟡 (measurement)?** The bias of the heartbeat-counting task is documented (Zamariola 2018); many of the indices used as an "interoception score" must therefore be read with caution. The field is still searching for better instruments.

> **If refuted, what survives?** Even if the measurement tools are abandoned entirely, anatomy and clinical observation stand: the interdependencies among the interoceptive map, emotion regulation and decision are supported by independent evidence. The dossier rests not on the measurement debate but on the functional claim.

## 6. Practical Implications and the Gateway Bridge

1. **The body is a sensor; emotion is a label.** Instead of "suppressing" emotions, **labelling** the body signal is a trainable skill. This is the physiological ground of modern emotion-regulation protocols.
2. **What the measurement measures: attention.** Interoception test outcomes depend on where attention is directed — and this holds in daily life: the body region attention turns to has its signal amplified.
3. **Gateway and interoception.** Body scan, breath awareness and relaxation stages produce a definable attention amplification. The order of these stages matters: the channel opens first, then deepens.
4. **Caution.** Excessive focus on the heartbeat **raises** anxiety in some people (especially in panic disorder and hypochondriasis); interoceptive training must therefore not be applied without regard for the person's anxiety profile. This is an important clinical finding showing that "more interoception is always better" is false.
