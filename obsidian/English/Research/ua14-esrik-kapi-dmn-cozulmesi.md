Why do they stand apart? Because merging them produces a sentence that disturbs both mystics and reductionists: **the content of the experience does not come from outside; when the filter lifts, the cognitive architecture's own substrate becomes visible.** This leaves both "the gods appeared" and "it is just a chemical error" insufficient.

## 2. Anomaly Inventory (with citations)

### 2.1 Visual constants: the quartet

Klüver (1926, *Mescal and Mechanisms of Hallucinations*) described four recurring geometric classes in mescaline experiences: **spirals**, **tunnels/funnels**, **cobwebs and lattice patterns**, **honeycomb/hexagonal patterns**. This quartet has been reported again and again across decades, substances, cultures and laboratories.

Bressloff, Cowan, Golubitsky, Thomas and Wiener (2001, *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A* 360:837–864) derived these constants from the **geometry of primary visual cortex (V1)**: V1's hypercolumnar structure and cortical connection symmetries generate these four patterns as "eigenmodes" at low activation threshold. The substance does not **add** an image; it exposes the architecture's **fundamental modes**.

### 2.2 Falling DMN gain: the most consistent pharmacological finding

Carhart-Harris et al. (2012, *PNAS* 109:2138–2143) showed decreased blood flow in medial prefrontal and posterior cingulate cortex (DMN hubs) under psilocybin. The dose-response relationship is also strongest for this network: as within-DMN connectivity falls, subjective "ego dissolution" rises.

Later work found a dose-mediated relationship between reduced within-DMN connectivity and **experience intensity** (Carhart-Harris et al., 2014, *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 8:20; Muthukumaraswamy et al., 2013, *Journal of Neuroscience* 33:15171–15183). This is the pharmacological wing of HP-2 (subtractive opening): **gain falls, content rises.**

### 2.3 The theoretical frame: REBUS

Carhart-Harris and Friston (2019, *Pharmacological Reviews* 71:316–344) formulated the mechanism in "REBUS and the Anarchic Brain": psychedelics lower the **precision weight** of high-level prior beliefs, so lower-level sensory data gain more say. The experience produces not "more information" but **fewer assumptions** — exactly what MP-2 (uncertainty duality) and HP-12 (entropy gradient) predict.

### 2.4 Mystical experience measurement: well designed but small samples

Pahnke (1962, Harvard dissertation), in the "Good Friday" experiment, reported that students given psilocybin during a service scored high on mystical experience scales; Doblin (1991, *Journal of Transpersonal Psychology*) confirmed the pattern by interviewing participants 25 years later. Griffiths et al. (2006, *Psychopharmacology* 187:268–283) showed that psilocybin in a controlled setting produced "personally meaningful and spiritually significant" experiences whose scores persisted at 14 months.

**An honest caution:** samples are small, participants are mostly experience-open volunteers, and blinding is technically difficult with these substances. Nevertheless the scales are standard and the effects large.

### 2.5 Entity encounters: verifiable?

"Entity" motifs frequently reported in DMT experiences (Strassman, 2001, *DMT: The Spirit Molecule*) repeat across cultures. But this does not mean the **content** is verified: the brain, as a social species, gives high prior weight to the "other mind" template; when the filter drops, that very template takes the stage. This does not diminish the experience but leaves its ontological status **untestable** — and by this dossier's principle, untestable content claims cannot reach law status.

### 2.6 Microdosing: a natural experiment with a clear result

Microdosing is a widespread do-it-yourself practice. But controlled, placebo-controlled studies (Szigeti et al., 2021, *eLife* 10:e62897; Cavanna et al., 2022, *Translational Psychiatry* 12:277) found **no cognitive benefit separable from expectancy**: perceived benefit is large, measured benefit absent. This is a fine test of HZ-13 (the meaning signature): when expectancy is strong, the subjective report is strong too.

### 2.7 Cultural convergence: the same plants, the same techniques

A shared pattern across unconnected cultures: (i) **two-component recipes** such as the DMT plant plus an MAO-inhibitor plant (ayahuasca); (ii) ritual framing, music, guidance (that is, HZ-13's signature production); (iii) meaning-making of the experience within a community. Traditional use thus operated the substance not **alone** but as part of a **protocol** — and modern therapeutic protocols arrived at the same conclusion (set and setting, preparation, integration).

### 2.8 The clinical and risk side

Psilocybin-assisted therapy has produced promising results for depression (Carhart-Harris et al., 2021, *NEJM* 384:1402–1411; Goodwin et al., 2022, *Nature Medicine* 28:844–851) — but these are clinically supervised protocols combined with **supported psychotherapy**, not a recommendation for unsupervised use. Risks include acute anxiety/panic, triggering in people with a psychosis history, HPPD and persistent perceptual disorders.

## 3. Three-Level Excavation

### Level 1 — Observation
- The four geometric constants recur independently of substance and culture.
- Psychedelics reduce DMN hub activity and within-network connectivity; the reduction relates to experience intensity.
- The precision weight of high-level priors falls (REBUS).
- Mystical experience scores are high and durable; samples are small.
- Microdosing does not separate from expectancy in controlled studies.
- The cross-cultural pattern is substance plus protocol.

### Level 2 — Pattern
A two-component pattern: **(A) One lock.** Different substances acting through different receptors hit the same network (DMN) and produce the same outputs. The "psychedelic experience" is therefore a function not of the substance but of **the door being unlocked**. **(B) Substrate exposure.** The visual constants are not content added by the drug but V1's eigenmodes; ego dissolution is not added content but the narrator being **turned down**. The psychedelic shows no picture: it lifts the projection screen and reveals the **machine room** behind it.

### Level 3 — Why is it designed this way?
Because the brain's job is not to show the world raw but to produce a **livable estimate**. V1 does not pass raw signal but filters that care about edges and textures; the DMN builds the autobiographical narrative and protects it with precision weighting. These two layers keep the organism alive — but they also **hide the architecture itself**.

When filter gain falls, the system is exposed to its own intermediate layers — which is why these experiences feel both **strikingly regular** (geometry, symmetry) and **irresistibly "real"**: intermediate layers are not what the upper layer normally sees, so the system takes the signal for "the outer world". Secrecy, then, is a function of the architecture; exposure is that secrecy temporarily lifting.

This also explains why traditions developed a "the veil lifted" language: what they observed really was a veil lifting — but behind the veil are not gods but **the architecture itself**. The interpretation of content varies with culture, listener and era; the **mechanism** does not.

## 4. HIDDEN PATTERN CANDIDATE: HZ-14 — Second Key, One Lock (Substrate Exposure)

> **Candidate Law (HZ-14).** Every route that lowers the gain of the narrative filter (DMN) — chemical, focus-based, sensory deprivation, rhythmic driving — produces the same class of outputs: (i) the cognitive architecture's eigenmodes becoming visible (form constants), (ii) the autobiographical narrator being turned down (ego dissolution), (iii) falling precision weight of high-level priors (entropy increase). **The substance adds no content; when the lock opens, the substrate shows.**

Simplified expression:

**D_report ≈ (1 / Π_DMN) × E_architecture** &nbsp;&nbsp; where Π_DMN is filter gain and E_architecture is the eigenmode density of the exposed layer.

**Three predictions:**
1. **Method independence:** form constants and ego dissolution should appear at similar rates under **every** method that lowers DMN gain (psilocybin ≈ DMT ≈ LSD ≈ deep focus ≈ sensory deprivation ≈ strong hypnagogia).
2. **Intensity = gain drop, not dose.** The effect of dose should be **mediated** by the DMN index; the same dose produces different intensity in people with different DMN suppression.
3. **Content cultural, form universal:** the same visual constants should be attached to entirely different narratives in different cultures (and they are).

**Discriminating test (HZ-14/1).** A four-arm comparison: (A) psilocybin (clinical dose), (B) experienced Gateway practitioners at Focus 15–21, (C) sensory deprivation (Ganzfeld/float), (D) the hypnagogic sleep-wake threshold. Shared measures: DMN index (fMRI/EEG), a standard form-constant inventory, an ego-dissolution scale, the mystical experience scale. Prediction: form-constant reports **distribute equally**; narrative content varies by method; intensity correlates with DMN drop independent of method.

**Second test (HZ-14/2 — substrate exposure).** Use transcranial magnetic stimulation to drive specific V1 symmetry modes and check whether the driven mode matches the constant reported during the experience. Prediction: mode-driven and drug experiences meet in the **same phenomenological class**.

**Third test (HZ-14/3 — weight or entropy?).** Under psychedelics, test mediation between increases in cortical activity entropy (Lempel-Ziv or sample entropy) and ego-dissolution scores. Prediction: entropy increase **mediates** the relationship between DMN drop and ego dissolution.

## 5. Evidence Thermometer: 🟢 (pharmacology and visual constants) / 🟡 (mysticism and therapy claims)

**Why 🟢?** DMN gain reduction has been shown with dose-response relationships in multiple studies; the derivation of form constants from V1 geometry is mathematically developed and their cross-cultural recurrence documented; clinical trials (NEJM, Nature Medicine) were run under controlled protocols.

**Why not a perfect score?** (1) Mystical experience samples are small and self-selected; (2) blinding is difficult; (3) which component drives therapeutic effect (substance, supported psychotherapy, expectancy) has not been decomposed; (4) microdosing, contrary to popular belief, does not separate in controlled studies; (5) entity encounters do not produce testable content claims.

> **If refuted, what survives?** If HZ-14's "one lock" claim collapses, what remains: the pharmacological evidence of the DMN-filter relationship, the architectural origin of form constants, and the clinical importance of "protocol + substance". This dossier also connects to the wider layer: **HZ-13 signature, HZ-14 lock, HZ-05 filter** — together they complete the "access architecture" family.

## 6. Practical Implications and the Gateway Bridge

1. **A second key is not a second outcome.** Opening the same lock does not guarantee the same result: the chemical key is fast and violent, the focus key slow and trainable. The difference lies in **control** and side-effect profile, not in the class of output.
2. **Training takes the key.** Dissolution reached by the focus route can be recalled voluntarily and does not traumatize; the chemical route, used without protocol, can traumatize. This answers "why is practice different from a drug": **repeatability is tied to skill rather than dosage.**
3. **Set and setting = signature verification.** The ritual framing of traditions and the preparation/integration phases of modern therapeutic protocols perform the signature function HZ-13 predicts. This is a condition of safety and efficacy, not decoration.
4. **Caution.** Psychedelics are controlled substances in many countries; they interact seriously with a personal or family history of psychosis, bipolar disorder, cardiovascular disease and certain medications. This dossier contains no recommendation for any substance use; clinical use occurs only under approved protocols and supervision.
5. **Research note:** this file forms a "state engineering" family with UA-21 (Orch-OR), UA-29 (retrocausality) and UA-39 (OBE neuro-anatomy); UA-10 (mythology) and UA-31 (trickster) complete the cultural face of narrative content.
