## 2. Anomaly Inventory (with sources)

### 2.1 Fascia: the line between what is real and what is embellished

**What is real (measured):**

- The fascia **is densely innervated**: Ruffini and Pacini corpuscles, and especially **type III/IV interstitial receptors**; their stimulation influences local blood flow and autonomic tone (Schleip, *Journal of Bodywork and Movement Therapies*, 2003, two-part "Fascial plasticity").
- **Myofibroblasts** (transformed fibroblasts carrying smooth-muscle actin) exist and **can contract**: Schleip et al. (2005) measured an average force of **0.02 N/cm²** in human lumbar fascia samples (in vitro, 12 samples); pharmacological stimulation increased contraction by ~25%.
- In animal models, fascia transmits **up to ~30%** of mechanical force.

**What is embellished (not measured):**

- Scale is decisive: striated muscle produces **hundreds of Newtons** per square centimetre; myofibroblast contraction is **~10,000 times weaker** and takes **minutes**. So the sentence "fascia contracts independently and creates movement itself" contradicts physiology's **orders of magnitude**.
- A 2025 review (myofascial continuity) notes that human evidence for myofascial force transmission is **limited**, samples are small (mean ~15), and 12 studies were of **low** methodological quality. Frameworks like "Anatomy Trains" and "tensegrity" are **descriptive models**, not validated pathways.
- The "sudden release" felt after manual therapy, per Schleip's own hypothesis, is not mechanical deformation but **mechanoreceptor → autonomic viscosity change** — a hypothesis, not a proven mechanism.

### 2.2 Spontaneous movement: kriya and relatives

**The phenomenology is real and cross-cultural:** shaking, twitching, swaying, spontaneous asana/mudra in meditation; "kriya" in Kriya Yoga terminology; "spontaneous qigong" in qigong; "St Vitus tremors" in the Christian line. Reports include twitching, tremor, breath surges, heat waves, tears/laughter discharges.

**Candidate mechanisms (ordinary and measurable):**

1. **Autonomic arousal excess:** as sympathetic tone drops during deep relaxation, the muscle-tremor threshold falls; "discharge" movements are common as anxiety releases.
2. **Respiratory alkalosis:** rapid/high-volume breathing (breathwork) lowers blood CO₂; result: **tingling, tetany, spasm, contraction** — what is experienced as "energy" is a near-verbatim description of this transient electrolyte picture. This is the most concrete physiological anchor of the kriya debate.
3. **Fasciculation/myoclonus/sleep-wake boundary:** hypnagogic jerks occur in everyone; meditation holds this boundary open for long periods.
4. **Expectancy and motor learning:** the instruction to "let go" implicitly **installs the expectation** of movement; as attention turns inward, the produced micro-movement is amplified by this expectancy (motor set + self-fulfilling attention).
5. **Dissociation and FND:** in intensive practice (long retreats, sleep deprivation, hyperventilation), the **functional movement disorder** spectrum appears. FND is a genuine neurological condition — not malingering — but its mechanism is "unconscious involuntary movement," not "hidden energy."

**Clinical line:** "Kundalini syndrome" case literature and descriptive instruments such as the **Physio-Kundalini Syndrome Index** (Greyson) exist; they **classify the experience**; they do not measure an energy channel.

### 2.3 Collective spread: from dancing plagues to tics

- **1518 Strasbourg dancing plague:** 50–400 people danced for weeks; John Waller's analysis is **mass psychogenic illness**: years of famine + plague fear + the belief in a **St Vitus "curse."** The ergotism thesis is rejected (symptoms do not match; outbreaks follow the Rhine/Moselle line but climate and crops differed) — Waller's discussion in *The Lancet*.
- **Le Roy 2011:** tic-like movements in dozens of teenage girls at a New York high school; neurologists' assessment: **conversion disorder/FND** + spread via social media.
- **TikTok tics (2021+):** functional tic-like outbreaks appearing with social-media exposure, described in the clinical literature as a distinct picture. The key point: spread follows the **dose of visual exposure** — precisely what mass psychogenic models predict.

## 3. Three-Level Excavation

### Level 1 — Observation

We observe: (a) the fascia carries dense sensory innervation and produces weak, slow, chemically triggered contraction; (b) kriyas are reported cross-culturally but their mechanism descriptions coincide with ordinary physiology (alkalosis, arousal discharge, the hypnagogic boundary); (c) FND is real, common, and not malingering; (d) mass psychogenic illness spreads via visual/social exposure in both historical and contemporary cases.

### Level 2 — Pattern

The pattern is this: **"release" is not the opening of an energy channel, but the discharge of arousal excess at the moment control is relaxed.** The formula therefore contains no fascia term; it contains excess, control, and **social permission**.

The second pattern: **fascia is the most easily marketed name for the "mystical network."** Scientific fact (dense innervation, mechanoreception, myofascial continuity) turns in popular accounts into **"the body's second brain / energy line"** — a leap the evidence cannot carry. This is the textbook case of HZ-30, epistemic immunity.

### Level 3 — Why is it like this?

Why do people experience kriya as "energy"? Because the **phenomenology** and the **mechanism** of the experience live at different levels: the movement feels not-self-generated (involuntariness), is accompanied by heat/tingling (alkalosis/autonomic), and ends in deep relief (parasympathetic return). This package almost forces the description "something flowed inside me." Second reason: the cultural frame **names the experience before it is interpreted** (kundalini, qi, chi) — the label shapes perception.

Third layer: why is collective spread so strong? Because humans are a species **wired for social learning**; a movement starting in one body is transmitted by being seen. This requires no "field": the **visual channel** suffices.

## 4. HIDDEN PATTERN CANDIDATE: HZ-20 — Release Threshold (not fascia, but autonomic excess)

> **Candidate law (HZ-20).** Spontaneous movement appears as the product of (arousal load − control capacity) excess with the withdrawal of voluntary monitoring and social permission; the fascia contributes as a **sensory and tension network**, but does not "create" the movement.

**H_kriya ≈ (A_arousal − K_control) × (1 − D_monitoring) × S_social** &nbsp;&nbsp;(A = autonomic/alkalotic load, K = voluntary control capacity, D = voluntary monitoring, S = social permission/exposure)

**Three predictions:**
1. **Alkalosis contribution:** when respiratory rate is clamped (normocapnia), kriya frequency drops significantly; the hyperventilation arm increases it. Kriya is a sensitivity to **carbon dioxide and muscle threshold**, not to "energy."
2. **Control arm:** a "suppress" instruction reduces kriya but increases inner distress; a "permit" instruction increases it — the variable is not a hidden flow but the **control set**.
3. **Social permission:** kriya rates are lower in solitary sitters than in group sessions; kriya/tic rates rise with the dose of video exposure (the pattern seen in TikTok tic outbreaks).

**Discriminating test (HZ-20/1 — preregistered retreat study, three arms).** In a meditation retreat participants are assigned to: (a) free release, (b) suppression, (c) respiratory clamping (normocapnia). Measures: EMG movement count/amplitude, capnography (end-tidal CO₂), heart-rate variability, and experience scales. Prediction: arm (c) significantly reduces kriya compared with arm (a).

**Second test (HZ-20/2 — fascial specificity).** In the same participants, myofascial manipulation (fascia-targeted manual therapy) is compared with sham manipulation; prediction: kriya rate **does not change** — because kriya is determined by arousal/control variables, not by fascia.

**Third test (HZ-20/3 — social exposure dose).** Healthy participants are exposed to measured doses of kriya/tic videos (0, 5, 15 minutes); prediction: a dose-response relation — movement-like behaviours increase with dose (functional movement spread), and this increase is moderated by the **individual's expectancy score**.

## 5. Evidence Thermometer: 🟢 / 🟡 / 🟠 / 🔴

**Why 🟢?** The dense innervation of fascia and its mechanical-sensory role are measured; myofibroblast contraction is demonstrated in the laboratory (including its scale).

**Why 🟡?** Kriya phenomenology is real and cross-cultural — but its evidence is **reports**; laboratory measurement (with EMG/capnography) is sparse.

**Why 🟠?** The collective-spread model (mass psychogenic illness) is retrospective and strong in historical cases; contemporary cases (Le Roy, TikTok) show the prospective pattern — but single historical events can never be diagnosed with certainty.

**Why 🔴?** "Fascial self-program," "energy body," "releasing blockages through the network": no measurement, no defined mechanism, no predictions.

> **If it is falsified, what remains?** If HZ-20/1 and /3 explain kriya through control and exposure, **two real and valuable things** remain: (1) **the fascia is a sensory organ** — proprioception, pain, and the clinical effect of manual therapy remain serious research areas in this frame; (2) **the clinical importance of involuntary movements** — FND diagnosis and treatment (explanatory diagnosis + physiotherapy/CBT) is evidence-based. In the other direction: if the respiratory-clamping arm does not reduce kriya, the "energy" narrative wins its first serious experimental support.

## 6. Practical Implications and the Gateway Bridge

1. **The right promise:** when the body relaxes, movements may appear; this is **normal**, mostly harmless and transient. A practice should frame this not as "energy discharge" but as **"autonomic balance return."**
2. **Safety (primary):** involuntary movements require **medical evaluation** when: new onset, one-sided, accompanied by altered consciousness, continuous/escalating, painful, or disrupting daily function. In breathwork, **hyperventilation** is contraindicated (fainting, tetany, panic); a practice must set an upper limit on breathing rate.
3. **FND language:** for users with functional movement disorder, the right message is: "This is real; you are not malingering; there is treatment." An app must not reverse this with "energy body" language — that language reinforces the symptom and delays access to treatment.
4. **Caution.** Courses/packages titled "fascial release," "kundalini activation," "releasing energy blockages" are the commercial form of the leap this file is about. The soothing effect of manual therapy is real; **its mechanism is not "blockage."**
5. **Research note:** this file links to HZ-36 (interoception), UA-25 (respiration/thermoregulation), and HZ-25 (autonomic root cipher); in the Φ-2 synthesis, kriya's common markers with HZ-10's (state shift) hypnagogic signatures will be tested.
