# UA-07 — Astro-Geophysical Coupling: The 13:30 Local Sidereal Time Anomaly

This file examines the claim that "anomalous cognition (psi) performance rises roughly fourfold around thirteen-thirty **local sidereal time** — not local clock time". The verdict has three parts: (1) **the archive really does show such a peak,** and its use of sidereal time formally rules out circadian rhythms and working-hour confounds; (2) **the peak rests on a contested base effect** — it is a data-driven window, has no independent prospective replication, and no measurement supports the "galactic gateway" mechanism; (3) the honest reading is: **the sidereal hour may be a geomagnetic gate, or it may be an artefact of one very large archive** — only a pre-registered experiment can tell.

## 1. Intersection Map: Astrophysics, Geomagnetism, Chronobiology and Experimental Psi Statistics All Look at the Same Hour

Four lines cross here:

1. **Astrophysics / celestial mechanics** — local sidereal time (LST): Earth's rotation relative to the stars; it slips about four minutes per day against the solar clock.
2. **Geomagnetism** — the state of the magnetosphere: `ap`/Kp indices, solar-wind proton density and speed.
3. **Chronobiology** — the separation of circadian (solar) from sidereal rhythms; melatonin and autonomic cycles.
4. **Experimental psi statistics** — archive meta-analyses of ganzfeld, remote-viewing and free-response trials.

Why do they not merge? Because merging produces a sentence both sides dislike: **if the peak is real, its carrier is most likely not "the stars" but Earth's magnetic weather; and if the peak is illusory, the lesson lies in the epistemology of archive analysis, not in astronomy.**

## 2. The Anomaly Inventory (Documented)

### 2.1 Spottiswoode 1997: a 13:30 peak across 1,468 trials

**Spottiswoode (1997, *Journal of Scientific Exploration* 11:1–17)** analysed **1,468 trials** pooled from different laboratories and different free-response protocols (ganzfeld, remote viewing): effect size showed a distinct peak around **13.5 h LST**, and outside the peak the correlation dropped to zero. He replicated the finding in an **independent validation sample of 1,015 trials** at the same hour. In the follow-up (**Spottiswoode & May**) the database grew to **2,879 trials** and the peak held; the window was defined as roughly **four hours** (about 17% of the sidereal day). The authors argued that this narrow window explains why individual studies run blind to the result failed to replicate: their sessions were simply not scheduled inside it. A similar pattern was reported in PEAR remote-viewing data.

The definition of LST matters: sidereal time slips about four minutes per day against solar time, so "13:30" is neither an office hour nor a circadian phase. This is the anomaly's strongest feature: **it cannot be explained by ordinary daily cycles.**

### 2.2 The candidate mediator: geomagnetism and solar wind

In an earlier analysis Spottiswoode (1997a) had reported that free-response effect size correlated with the **`ap` geomagnetic index**. Spottiswoode & May also examined solar-wind parameters and argued that the relationship appears **only inside the LST window**, vanishing outside it. This yields a **"magnetospheric window"** model rather than a "stellar gateway": 13.5 h LST is not a celestial direction but a slice of the magnetosphere's orientation relative to the stars.

### 2.3 The counter-evidence chain (honesty record)

1. **The base effect is contested.** The LST anomaly presupposes that "anomalous cognition" is real. **Milton and Wiseman (1999, *Psychological Bulletin* 125:387–391)** meta-analysed the 30 ganzfeld studies from 7 independent laboratories that met strict methodological criteria: participants did not score above chance (Stouffer z = 0.70; p = 0.24); only one of the internal effects reported by Bem & Honorton was confirmed, and the original significance claim was found to be erroneous. The positive wing of the field (Bem & Honorton 1994; Storm & Tressoldi 2020 and later) maintains that the effect persists. The LST peak therefore sits **inside a polarised literature.**
2. **The window is data-driven.** The 13.5 h peak was not predicted; it was found by scanning a large archive. Any analysis that searches a 24-hour circle for a peak will find one; the real question is whether the peak reappears **at the same hour in independent data** — and that has been shown only once.
3. **The prospective attempt is weak.** A prospective LST study on Sheldrake's telephone-telepathy data (peak vs off-peak sessions) reported "weak support": the sample was small, many sessions irregular, and power was inadequate. It points in the direction of the effect but cannot decide — inconclusive.
4. **No independent, pre-registered, high-powered replication exists.** The decisive test has been awaited for decades.

### 2.4 Mainstream line: small but real geomagnetism–physiology associations

Independent of the LST claim, **small and mixed** associations between geomagnetic storms and human physiology have been reported: some studies find that geomagnetic disturbances reduce nocturnal melatonin metabolite (Burch et al.), others report a tendency for autonomic rhythms to synchronise with magnetic activity (McCraty et al. 2017). None of these is "psi" and effect sizes are small — but they do refute the sentence "the magnetosphere never affects physiology". If the peak is real, this is the most likely channel.

## 3. Three-Level Excavation

### Level 1 — Observation

What is observed: (a) a 13.5 h LST peak in a 1,468-trial archive; (b) the same peak in a 1,015-trial validation sample; (c) a roughly four-hour window across 2,879 trials; (d) a similar pattern reported in PEAR data; (e) weak support from a prospective telephone-telepathy attempt; (f) the base ganzfeld effect missing in one meta-analysis and persisting in others; (g) small real geomagnetism–physiology associations.

### Level 2 — Pattern

The pattern is this: **two rival explanations fit the same data, and the current evidence cannot separate them.**

- **Physical reading:** 13.5 h LST is a slice of magnetospheric orientation; inside that slice a very small neural modulation (melatonin/autonomic tone) makes marginal psi performance visible. On this reading the peak is not a "stellar gate" but a **"magnetic weather window"**.
- **Statistical reading:** a large, heterogeneous, publication-biased archive + peak-hunting on a 24-hour circle + a window selected after the fact → a structured illusion. On this reading the peak is not durable.

Second pattern: **the peak is "interesting" precisely because it is sidereal rather than circadian — and that is also what makes it expensive.** A sidereal modulation demands a geophysical mediator; it cannot lean on any easy everyday-life explanation.

### Level 3 — Why is it like this?

Why has this anomaly stayed unresolved for so long? Because resolving it requires an **expensive experiment**: a high-powered, pre-registered ganzfeld study run in peak and control LST slices — while the base effect itself is contested. Nobody wants to fund a study whose every outcome yields little credit. Second reason: **archive analyses can be done after the fact; prospective experiments cannot** — the field has learned to live on past data.

Third layer: this file shows the program's **epistemic immunity** theme (HZ-30) in its purest form. The difference between "interesting statistic" and "real effect" is not the size of the data but **whether the test was written in advance.**

## 4. HIDDEN PATTERN CANDIDATE: HZ-07 — Sidereal-Hour Window (geomagnetic mediator candidate)

> **Candidate Law (HZ-07, revised).** In anomalous-cognition archives, effect size rises markedly around 13.5 h LST; the window is sidereal, not circadian. The mechanism, however, is not a "galactic gateway" but a candidate **geomagnetic/ionospheric mediator**; the window was data-driven and has no independent prospective support.

Simplified expression:

**E(LST) ≈ E_0 × [1 + W(LST; 13.5 h, σ ≈ 2 h)] × (1 + β·M_ap)**, where W is a window function and M_ap the magnetic-activity term; **if β = 0 the window is a statistical artefact.**

**Three predictions:**
1. **Prospective obligation:** a pre-registered, power-analysed study comparing peak and control LST slices must **reproduce the ×4 claim**; if it does not, the anomaly descends to a database artefact.
2. **Mediator trace:** effect size inside the window must **stratify** with `ap`/solar-wind parameters; outside the window the relationship must vanish.
3. **Specificity:** when trial times are shifted by a random sidereal offset, the peak must disappear; the peak must **not** lock onto solar clock time (blind re-analysis).

**Discriminating test (HZ-07/1 — pre-registered ganzfeld, LST arms).** ~1,000+ trials per arm at peak (13.5 ± 2 h LST) and control (01.5 ± 2 h LST) times, powered ≥ 0.9 for the claimed effect size; primary outcome: anomaly score; secondary: `ap` stratification. This experiment declares the anomaly either alive or dead.

**Second test (HZ-07/2 — mediator probe).** In existing archives, the in-window effect-size relationship with `ap`/solar-wind parameters is analysed under a pre-registered statistical plan with publication-bias models; off-window slices serve as negative controls.

**Third test (HZ-07/3 — specificity and blind shift).** Trial times are re-labelled with tens of thousands of random sidereal shifts while experiment identity is hidden; the peak must be an **extreme value** in the shift distribution — otherwise the window is a multiple-comparison artefact.

## 5. Evidence Thermometer: 🟡 / 🟠 / 🔴

**Why 🟡?** The archive pattern appears in two independent samples and its sidereal nature excludes easy explanations; that is a noteworthy statistical fact.

**Why 🟠?** Prospective evidence is weak (a small, underpowered telephone-telepathy attempt) and the base effect the peak sits on has failed one meta-analysis; the window is data-driven.

**Why 🔴?** Mechanism claims such as "galactic window" or "stellar gateway": no measurement, no defined carrier, no predictions. The geomagnetic mediator, by contrast, is **testable** — hence a candidate, not a 🔴.

> **What remains if it is refuted?** If HZ-07/1 comes back null, the LST peak descends to an archive illusion and what remains is a **valuable methodological lesson**: hunting windows in large archives does not produce evidence without pre-registration. In the other direction: if the peak is prospectively confirmed, the small real effects in the geomagnetism–physiology line (melatonin, autonomic tone) gain new weight and join UA-22's "silent magnetic signature" file. In both cases, practical advice of the "meditate at 13:30" kind remains unsupported.

## 6. Practical Implication and Gateway Bridge

1. **The honest promise:** cosmic-timing narratives ("the field opens at certain hours") are exciting, but in this file they **carry no evidence**. The app's timing layer should lean on circadian chronobiology (sleep schedules, light hygiene, session times), not on sidereal time.
2. **What is real?** Time of day genuinely affects sleep and attention — but that concerns the **solar** clock and its mechanism is known (the circadian system).
3. **Magnetic weather:** the physiological effects of geomagnetic activity are a small but real research line; the app should track it as a **research note**, not market it as a feature.
4. **Warning.** Apps that sell subscriptions framed around "cosmic timing" and "stellar gateways" are exactly the kind this file exists to prevent: packaging an unmeasured mechanism as if it were measured. No user-facing promise should be built on these findings.
5. **Research note:** this file links with UA-27 (psi meta-analyses) and HZ-30 (epistemic immunity) into the "evidence hygiene" family, and with UA-22 (magnetic sense) into the "geophysics–neural" line. The Φ-2 synthesis will test possible shared geophysical variables between the LST window and HZ-24 (sleep synchronisation) and HZ-25 (autonomic root key).
