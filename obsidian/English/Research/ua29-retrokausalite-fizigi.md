# UA-29 — Retrocausality Physics: What Happens at the Time Boundary?

In a delayed-choice experiment, does a later decision change the past? And might a separation in skin conductance a few seconds before stimulus presentation be the human counterpart of that physics? **It is too early either to dismiss both or to join them.** Time-symmetric quantum formulae offer powerful conditional predictions for *pre- and post-selected* subsets, but ordinary delayed choice and quantum eraser experiments do not send information into the past. The autonomic "presentiment" literature contains both a positive meta-analysis and method-focused negative experiments. **No mechanism joining the two has been demonstrated.** The candidate pattern here is not a conclusion that time has broken; it is a testable boundary condition for **how conditioning on future data changes our description of earlier events**.

## 1. Intersection Map: Quantum Foundations × Weak Measurement × Autonomic Physiology

Four clocks operate here. **The quantum clock:** possible states between preparation and final measurement. **The experimental clock:** photodiodes, hardware randomness and actual on-screen stimulus onset; timestamps are critical. **The bodily clock:** skin conductance decays slowly, cardiac intervals are autocorrelated, and the preceding trial can leak into the next baseline. **The analytical clock:** choosing windows after knowing outcomes divides earlier data retrospectively into subclasses. The fourth can create patterns resembling the first without changing physics.

This dossier separates *mathematical dependence on a future condition* (P(intermediate | initial, final)), *operational backward signaling* (can a future free choice be read in the past?), and *human prediction* (can participants discriminate a trial's category above chance before its outcome?). Answers cannot be borrowed from one question to another.

## 2. Anomaly Inventory: Three Distinct Evidence Lines

### 2.1 Delayed choice and the quantum eraser

In Wheeler's delayed-choice design, the measurement arrangement can be changed after a photon enters its path. In Kim et al.'s (2000) quantum eraser a "signal" photon and a partner measured later are matched by their records. **Subsamples sorted afterward** by partner outcome can show interference and counter-interference; the **unsorted early signal-photon distribution** does not change. Reading the experiment as "the future photon redrew the earlier photograph" mistakes outcome-conditioned data selection for event dynamics. Ellerman (2015) calls this the *separation fallacy*. The phenomena are real, but demonstrate no readable message to the past.

The two-state-vector approach (Aharonov–Bergmann–Lebowitz, 1964) conditions an intermediate measurement on both the preparation and subsequently selected final state. Aharonov–Albert–Vaidman (1988) give the weak value as `A_w = ⟨φ|A|ψ⟩/⟨φ|ψ⟩`: when the denominator is small the pointer shift may exceed the operator's eigenvalue range. This is **a pointer statistic for a selected ensemble**, not one particle literally carrying 100 spin units. Time-symmetric interpretations (for example the transactional account) read the mathematics through different ontologies; current measurements alone do not choose among them.

### 2.2 The body's purported “sense of the future”

Mossbridge, Tressoldi & Utts (2012, *Frontiers in Psychology* 3:390; DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00390) meta-analyzed **26 reports** from 1978–2010; they reported an apparent small predictive physiological-activity effect, a pooled standardized effect of about **d ≈ 0.21**. The comparison was between physiological windows **before** randomly presented emotional and neutral stimuli. Their 2014 discussion directly addresses expectation, trial order and multiple analyses: the pro-effect literature should not be ignored.

On the critical side, Schwarzkopf (2014/2015) emphasizes slow baseline decay, residue from preceding stimuli, the gambler's fallacy, weak linear expectation controls and publication selection. "Investigating expectation effects using multiple physiological measures" (2015, *Frontiers in Psychology*, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2015.01553) found **no presentiment** under varied ordering conditions but did find **physiological expectation effects**; its authors explicitly caution that their different task does not automatically disprove every earlier positive study. The balance includes both a published small positive signal and ordinary mechanisms capable of creating it. A **large independent design-faithful, preregistered replication** is the decisive next step.

**Follow-up meta-analysis (2018):** Duggan & Tressoldi screened 19 newer studies and reported an overall positive sign, but pooled peer-reviewed and non-peer-reviewed material with substantial heterogeneity (I² around 82% for the expanded 27-experiment analysis). Author overlap and variable analytical choices mean this is not a substitute for a large independent preregistered multicenter test. Neither “the first meta-analysis was never followed up” nor “the debate is over” is accurate.

### 2.3 Why “fully randomized” may not be enough

Randomization *can* make the outcome independent of the past, but only with the right sequencing and timing. Without-replacement within-block presentation allows remaining target counts to be inferred. Even with independent generation on every trial, one must check fixed screen latency, preloaded sounds or images, predictable stimulus ratios, pre-trigger display frames, movement artifacts, post hoc choice of phase or short time windows and multiple tests. Randomization also cannot remove a connection to previous trials introduced by *baseline processing*. Raw physiological timestamps and hardware logs need protection against retrospective relocation of the "pre" window.

### 2.4 What would actually distinguish a temporal effect?

| Outcome | Delayed choice | Autonomic study | Necessary inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early subsets differ after final class is known | Normal post-selection | May be expectation/order artifact | **No** backward signal |
| Raw early marginal differs by a later free choice | Not predicted by standard QM | Candidate genuine foreknowledge | Blind independent replication |
| Participant discriminates single future trials sequentially | Does not follow from quantum interpretations | Testable behavioral claim | Locked threshold survives an external sample |
| Small p but strong sensitivity to window selection | Lower evidential weight | Possible data leakage | Adjusted analysis plus raw data |

**No-signalling** does not prohibit descriptions conditioned on later information; it rules out an *accessible earlier marginal* encoding a later free choice. That distinction does not trivialize the physics — it states precisely what has been observed.

## 3. Three-Level Excavation

**Level 1 — Observation:** weak values and delayed conditional correlations are reproducible in the laboratory; existing eraser data do not change a message-bearing early marginal. Presentiment has a positive meta-analysis; a 2015 methodological study found an alternative expectation trace.

**Level 2 — Hidden pattern:** "the future appears in the past" has two meanings: using later information to *classify past data*, versus a later decision *physically altering the past*. Changed conditional distributions suffice for the former. The same analysis-window pitfall applies to physiology: reweighting early signals once the future stimulus category is known is not equivalent to independent forward prediction.

**Level 3 — Historical flow:** Wheeler's thought experiment → the graphical allure of the quantum eraser → the weak value “100” headline → videos about the body's “future feeling.” Every step measures something else; the shared language of future influence can make them sound like one mechanism. Cross-disciplinary honesty preserves standard causality while remaining prepared to update if a genuine unexpected early marginal is detected.

## 4. HIDDEN PATTERN CANDIDATE: HZ-29 — Time-Symmetric Boundary Condition

> **Candidate law:** A later record can affect a *retrospectively constructed conditional distribution*; a retrocausal communication claim requires a further demonstration that an unknown later free choice affects the **blind earlier marginal**.

`P(X_early | S_final, I_initial) ≠ P(X_early | I_initial)` is possible; under standard conditions `P(X_early | do(S_final=0)) = P(X_early | do(S_final=1))`. Here `do` denotes a future free choice, not a mark carried into the past. This equality alone cannot settle the physical ontology between preparation and selection; it only tests an operational message claim.

**Discriminating test A (physics):** timestamp and cryptographically seal single-photon results; record the distant/final choice with a physically independent generator *afterward*. Primary endpoint is not an interference pattern in subsets but blind classification of the future arm from early data alone, before its labels are opened. A compatible null plus successful later conditional sorting is the standard prediction. In the absence of a reproducible, calibrated early marginal difference, there is no backward signal.

**Discriminating test B (physiology):** only obtain the stimulus class from an independent hardware generator after the "pre" window and the preceding-trial decay data are write-once sealed; variable intertrial intervals and *with-replacement* Bernoulli allocation. Preregister **one** channel (such as electrodermal activity), one window, one directed category difference, and raw data/model code. Model preceding stimulus, person-level variation, temporal autocorrelation, light/sound leakage; require blind external-center confirmation and an uncertainty interval. Even positive net discrimination **would not automatically follow from the two-state-vector formalism**: an additional mechanism and scale bridge is needed.

**Decision rule:** the prespecified primary effect's uncertainty interval excludes zero in the external sample and the confound controls are negative; otherwise inconclusive/negative. After-the-fact selection of "sensitive" subjects is not accepted. Rejection criteria for each interpretation are declared ahead of time.

## 5. Evidence Thermometer and Falsification Box

**🟢** Delayed-choice correlations, the conditional ABL/TSVF calculus and weak-measurement pointer effects are laboratory facts. **🟡** A small pooled positive effect has been published in the presentiment meta-analysis; method heterogeneity and the need for independent confirmation remain substantial. **🟠** Operational backward-in-time information, or an autonomic pre-response caused by time-symmetric physics, has not been demonstrated; this dossier's overall slot is here. **🔴** The claim that consciousness freely selects the future and transmits messages into the past, supposedly proven by a quantum eraser, is an invalid inference.

> **If falsified, what remains?** If blind future-choice discrimination stays at chance, **conditional quantum probabilities and time-symmetric mathematics remain**; they are not evidence of human foreknowledge. If presentiment fails a large independent test, genuine *expectation physiology*, arousal adaptation and stimulus calibration remain worth studying. Even a real early marginal difference first demands elimination of experimental leakage; it does not automatically establish “consciousness changed the past.”

---

## 6. Practical Implications and the Gateway Bridge

Feeling that something is about to happen during meditation is a real *experience*, not proof of future knowledge. No “receive messages from the future” exercise or advice to base medical/financial decisions on intuitions follows. An advance-dated, independently verifiable single-event prediction in a journal helps reduce hindsight bias; it need not be marketed as a paranormal test. HZ-27's channel control, HZ-28's blinding and HZ-30's evidential weight turn into a shared temporal discipline here: **which information was accessible at precisely which time?**
