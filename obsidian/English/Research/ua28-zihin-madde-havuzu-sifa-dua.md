# UA-28 — The Mind-to-Matter Pool: Healing, Prayer and Random Event Generators

This file examines the question "does intention directly affect matter" along three lines of evidence: **random event generators (REG/micro-PK)** — an archive of millions of trials; **distant healing and therapeutic touch** — clinical trials and practitioner tests; and **intercessory prayer** — the largest randomised trials, run on bypass patients. The verdict has three parts: (1) **the archives contain small positive deviations, and those deviations have been declared "decisive" even at the scale of millions of trials** (PEAR: 2.5 million trials, 3.8 sigma); (2) **the deviation moves inversely with design quality** — the best-designed studies produce the smallest (mostly zero) effects; the independent three-laboratory REG replication is negative, the largest prayer trial (STEP) found nothing, and therapeutic-touch practitioners could not distinguish their own "energy fields"; (3) the honest verdict: **the archive carries an "experimenter–field dependence" signature** — the effect appears not in the world but in the measurement relation, which makes it investigable as **sociology and methodology**, not as physics.

## 1. Intersection Map: Experimental Physics, Medical Statistics, Sociology and Methodology All Look at the Same "Intention"

Four lines cross here:

1. **Experimental physics / electronic noise** — REGs, quantum-based random generators, coherence and noise physics.
2. **Medical statistics and clinical trials** — intercessory prayer, distant healing, therapeutic touch; triple-blind designs and survival outcomes.
3. **Sociology and the sociology of science** — laboratory communities, publication bias, experimenter expectancy, the institutional collapse of a field.
4. **Methodology** — pre-registration, multiple comparisons, Monte Carlo simulation, selective reporting.

Why do they not merge? Because merging produces a sentence both sides dislike: **the measured deviation is real but its carrier is not intention — it is the relation** — and the way to study a relation is not physics but methodology.

## 2. The Anomaly Inventory (Documented)

### 2.1 The REG line: PEAR's rise and the test that came from inside

- **Jahn and Dunne (1987, *Foundations of Physics*):** with 2.5 years of data at Princeton, a result of **6.35 sigma** was reported for human intention skewing REG output. This is the highest-claim moment of the micro-PK field.
- **The 12-year "benchmark" (Jahn, Dunne et al. 1997):** **2.5 million trials, 91 participants**, three protocol arms (high intention / low intention / baseline). The cumulative deviation was reported at **3.8 standard deviations**.
- **But the inside view appeared later:** re-assessments showed the result was dominated by **two extreme participants**: their personal databases stood at z = 5.6 and z = 3.4 and produced **about a quarter of the total data**. The "group effect" was thus largely the effect of **two people**.
- **The independent replication:** three laboratories ran a joint study with **750,000 trials per condition**, 227 participants, and a power estimate of ~85% based on the PEAR effect. The result was **non-significant**. The field's own explanation must also enter the file: the PEAR sample was **not homogeneous**; the power calculation optimistically assumed the effect of an average participant — i.e. the real effect was concentrated "in some operators". This does not save the claim; it reduces it to **operator specificity** (HZ-28 below).
- **The meta-analytic exam:** **Bösch, Steinkamp & Boller (2006, *Psychological Bulletin* 132:497–523)** combined 380 studies: a **small but significant** overall effect… with two warnings: (a) effect size was **inversely related** to sample size (small studies yield large effects), and (b) heterogeneity was extreme. A Monte Carlo simulation showed that **publication bias alone could produce this pattern**. Radin, Nelson and colleagues (2006) replied in the same issue that selective reporting was implausible; the journal published the debate alongside. An in-field reassessment later argued the meta-analysis contained data-coding/selection errors — **the dispute is unresolved**, but what is on record is this: the effect tends to shrink as quality control rises.
- **The scale test:** the largest-scale extensions (global REG networks searching for correlations with world events) produced many anecdote-level "relations"; however, because the **primary outcomes were not pre-defined and event selection remained free**, they cannot count as independent confirmation. In 2007 the PEAR laboratory closed; large-scale programmatic production ended.

### 2.2 Healing and therapeutic touch: the practitioner test

- **Rosa, Rosa, Sarner & Barrett (1998, *JAMA* 279:1005–1010):** therapeutic-touch (TT) practitioners were asked to **sense** which of their hands a child had placed her hand over, behind a screen. **Twenty-one experienced practitioners**, across 280 trials, scored **44%** — chance is 50%; that is, **below chance**. The authors' verdict: TT's foundational claim (a detectable "human energy field") is unsupported.
- **An honest addition:** later analyses showed the same design *should* produce above-chance accuracy via **heat**; when heat-blocking materials were interposed, accuracy returned **to chance**. People were detecting a temperature cue, not a "field" — a detail that strengthens the test rather than weakening it.
- **The distant-healing systematic review:** **Astin, Harkness & Ernst (2000, *Annals of Internal Medicine* 132:903–910)** reviewed 23 randomised trials (2,774 patients) and reported positive effects in 13 (57%); but methodological limitations prevented definitive conclusions: heterogeneity, weak blinding, freedom in outcome selection. This is the high point of the "positive archive" reading.

### 2.3 Intercessory prayer: the largest trials and what they say

- **Byrd (1988):** positive outcomes in coronary care patients — but with multiple-comparison and outcome-selection criticisms.
- **MANTRA II (Krucoff et al. 2001, n = 748) and Aviles et al. (2001, n = 799):** **no significant differences** in long-term outcomes.
- **Meta-analysis:** **Masters, Spielmans & Goodson (2006, *Annals of Behavioral Medicine* 32:21–26)**: 14 studies, pooled effect g = 0.100, **no different from zero**; removing one controversial study drops the effect to **g = 0.012**; no moderator explains the results. The follow-up (Masters & Spielmans 2007): "no discernible effect"; in the ill-only subgroup p = 0.09 (borderline), in healthy people p = 0.57.
- **Cochrane (Roberts et al. 2009):** no effect on death (OR 1.11; 95% CI 0.79–1.56).
- **STEP (Benson et al. 2006, *American Heart Journal* 151:934–942) — the single most important trial in this file:** 6 hospitals, **1,802 bypass patients**, three arms: (a) prayer, patient not told, (b) no prayer, nobody knows, (c) prayer, **patient told**. Result: **prayer itself did not affect complication-free recovery.** And the second finding: in the group that **knew** it was being prayed for, the complication rate was higher (59% vs 51–52%); the authors added that this could be chance. That second finding is the file's most instructive part: in the largest trial, **the only measurable effect was not the prayer but the expectancy around it** — and its sign was negative (a stress/nocebo direction).

### 2.4 The field's collapse signature

The file shows all three lines flowing the same way: claims shrink at large, well-controlled scales. PEAR closed in 2007; TT was shelved as an "energy field" in the clinical literature; intercessory prayer zeroed in its largest trial; the authors of the REG meta-analysis demonstrated by simulation that their own result could be produced by publication bias. This is a collapse signature in which the claims **did not die but shrank**: the effect moved from the 10⁻⁴ range towards "nothing" — and as it moved, **quality control rose.**

## 3. Three-Level Excavation

### Level 1 — Observation

What is observed: (a) small deviations across millions of REG trials; (b) the deviation concentrated in two extreme participants; (c) the independent three-laboratory replication negative; (d) in the meta-analysis the effect is inverse to sample size and consistent with a publication-bias simulation; (e) TT practitioners could not confirm their own foundational claim (44% < 50%), and their "sensing" turned out to depend on a heat cue; (f) the distant-healing review is mostly positive but methodologically weak; (g) prayer meta-analyses are zero; (h) the largest prayer trial is zero — plus a **harm signal in the expectancy arm**.

### Level 2 — Pattern

The pattern is this: **the effect moves inversely with design quality.** This is the strongest reason to say "there is no effect"; because a natural effect (gravity, radiation, a side effect) **grows** as quality rises, it does not shrink. In a regression-based model of "observed effect = real + bias", the shrinking of the observed effect as the quality term grows is best explained by assuming the **real term is ~0**.

Second pattern: **the field's own tests produced the hardest evidence.** The experiment that refuted TT was a child's homework; the prayer exam was run by **religious researchers**; the REG meta-analysis was published in the field's own journal with the counter-arguments in the same issue. This is the positive face of HZ-30's epistemic immunity: **evidence hygiene can coexist with belief.**

### Level 3 — Why is it like this?

Why are these claims so sticky? Because for a human being the belief "my intention has an effect" sits at the centre of the world model: the praying patient, the healing healer, the well-wishing relative. A finding that refutes this belief refutes not just a hypothesis but **a moral economy**. Second reason: effects at the 10⁻⁴ level sit in a region that is **statistically interesting but practically useless**; so both "there is evidence" and "it has no use" can be true — and each side spins the debate by showing its own half.

Third layer: STEP's **harm signal in the expectancy arm** is the file's most valuable clinical lesson. If knowing "I am being prayed for" produced a measurable physiological effect (stress, arrhythmia risk), then **expectancy is itself a medical variable** — and this is the science not of the supernatural but of **placebo/nocebo** (UA-13). The same mechanism explains the fine line between "hope of recovery" and "performance pressure".

## 4. HIDDEN PATTERN CANDIDATE: HZ-28 — Experimenter–Field Dependence

> **Candidate Law (HZ-28).** In anomalous mind-to-matter archives, the size of the observed deviation increases with (i) the experimenter's/operator's intention and belief, (ii) participant expectancy and (iii) protocol flexibility; it decreases as design quality (blinding, pre-registration, frozen outcome) rises. This pattern indicates that the effect is **a property of the measurement relation**, not of the world.

**Δ_observed ≈ (Σ_operators × N_intention × E_expectancy) / (1 + Q_design) − B_bias**

**Three predictions:**
1. **The quality gradient:** the same claim yields systematically decreasing effects at three quality levels (exploratory / blinded / pre-registered multi-centre); the gradient can be explained only by a genuine physical term if it does **not** zero out.
2. **The operator-transfer test:** "high-effect" operators such as PEAR's two extremes must reproduce the effect **in another laboratory, under a pre-registered protocol**. If they do, the effect is transferable (a person-bound ability); if not, the effect is bound to the **laboratory relation**.
3. **The expectancy sign:** in clinical arms, knowing "I am being seen" produces a measurable effect; that effect **need not be positive** (STEP). Prediction: when expectancy is manipulated, physiological measures change but **the target disease outcome does not.**

**Discriminating test (HZ-28/1 — adversarial-laboratory REG trial, pre-registered).** Two expert laboratories (believers in the effect) and two sceptical laboratories; a shared protocol; the **primary outcome frozen in advance**; ≥ 3 million trials in total; secondary output: within-laboratory effect variance and the transferability of the operator ranking. Prediction: the quality gradient reduces the effect to a laboratory term.

**Second test (HZ-28/2 — experimenter permutation).** The same subject pool, the same protocol, run with the **experimenter identity randomly varied** (believer/sceptic/blind); prediction: the effect covaries with the experimenter arm more than with the subject. This design is a direct, ethical and evidence-based exam of the hypothesis "psi = experimenter effect".

**Third test (HZ-28/3 — triple-blind healing protocol).** Distant healing: practitioner, patient and assessor blinded; target an **objective** primary outcome (e.g. length of hospital stay, a laboratory measure); pre-registration mandatory; prediction: no effect on the objective outcome, a small effect on subjective well-being — and that effect is **indistinguishable from the expectancy arm**.

## 5. Evidence Thermometer: 🟡 / 🟠 / 🔴

**Why 🟡?** The REG archive produced small, statistically significant deviations at a scale unprecedented in history (millions of trials); that is too large a database to dismiss with "there is nothing".

**Why 🟠?** But the same archive contains the inverse relation between effect and quality and the publication-bias simulation; the independent multi-laboratory replication is negative; the largest prayer trial and the practitioner tests are zero.

**Why 🔴?** Healing and prayer claims are **clinically and mechanistically** unsupported: no carrier, no dose-response, no effect under blinding. Moreover, STEP's expectancy arm showed that unregulated "healing promises" can produce **harm**.

> **What remains if it is refuted?** If HZ-28/1 and /3 zero the quality gradient (i.e. pre-registered adversarial trials do show a genuine effect), the program's "measurable but mechanism-less" layer stands up and the physics section would need to be rewritten (it would be Φ-2's most provocative outcome). In the other direction, four things remain **solid and valuable**: (1) **placebo/nocebo science** — the measurable physiological power of expectancy; (2) **blinding and pre-registration methodology** — the lessons from the field's own collapse; (3) **the ethics of care** — touch, attention and company work not in the name of an "energy" but in the name of **relationship**; (4) **statistical literacy** — what effects at the 10⁻⁴ level mean for careers, beliefs and capital.

## 6. Practical Implication and Gateway Bridge

1. **The honest promise:** no app can promise distant healing, healing through prayer, or "affecting matter with intention". This file closes that door with evidence.
2. **Clinical warning (primary):** healing/prayer practices cannot replace **evidence-based treatment**; delaying medical care produces measurable harm. STEP's expectancy arm shows that interventions dressed as "spiritual support" can carry a negative sign: **building expectancy pressure on a patient is not safe.**
3. **What is real:** the placebo response, subjective well-being, the care relationship and a sense of meaning are real and measurable effects — but their mechanisms are **expectancy, stress regulation, autonomic tone and relationship** (UA-13, UA-25).
4. **Application bridge:** the "healing" language of Gateway sessions should be translated into "relaxation, awareness and meaning"; the app must carry no medical claim.
5. **Research note:** this file should be read together with UA-27 (the same statistical generation and experimenter term) and UA-13 (the pharmacology of expectancy); in the Φ-2 synthesis, HZ-28's "quality gradient" criterion is a candidate **filter law** to be applied to **all** of the program's anomaly files (UA-07, UA-22, UA-27, UA-29 and others).
