# The road to the out-of-body experience — separation methods

Monroe's journey began with an out-of-body experience (OBE), yet the manual is honest: "the Institute does not guarantee an OBE." As the research progressed it became clear that participants enter distinct, recognisable states of consciousness, and the OBE is only one of them. Still, the series contains a clear path toward out-of-body work: Focus 10 → REBAL → Liftoff → First-Stage Separation → the energy-body exercises of Odyssey → Focus 21. This topic describes that path, its methods and what to expect.

## 1. The road map

1. **Ground — Focus 10 (Discovery):** mind awake, body asleep. The precondition of an OBE is that the body believes it is "asleep"; Focus 10 establishes that.
2. **Safety — REBAL (Advanced Focus 10):** building and reabsorbing the energy body. Learn the return before you separate.
3. **The sleep gate — Exploration, Sleep (Discovery #5):** keeping awareness while falling asleep and recalling in the morning. Monroe's own method, "borderland sleep", passes through here.
4. **Take-off — Liftoff (Freedom #1):** the sensation of "rising" from Focus 10 into 12; the first light feelings of leaving the body.
5. **Methods — First-Stage Separation (Freedom #6):** five basic separation methods; try one per session.
6. **Energy body — Odyssey #1–3:** Sensing (feeling the energy body), Expansion (extending it), Point of Departure (projecting "out of phase" with the body — the launch point).
7. **Bridge — Focus 21 (Odyssey #5–6):** crossing to other energy systems; from here the concept "out of body" becomes "consciousness at another focus".

## 2. The borderland-sleep method (Monroe, Journeys Out of the Body, chapter 16)

Monroe's book defines four **conditions**; the Hemi-Sync series "Support for Journeys Out of the Body" devotes one exercise to each:

- **Condition A:** keeping awareness at the sleep–waking border (the hypnagogic state). When images start to stream, watching them without getting caught.
- **Condition B:** when the images calm, holding awareness in the remaining "dark" field; feeling the body asleep.
- **Condition C:** the fading of bodily perception (weight, warmth, position); the question "where is my body?" goes unanswered.
- **Condition D:** the union of the previous three in a single exercise; the strongest signals of the series. From here one passes into the "vibrational state".

**The vibrational state:** a high-frequency vibration or hum felt throughout the body, as Monroe describes it. It can be frightening; fear cuts it off. Method: "roll the vibration through the body like a wave from head to foot", speed it up, smooth it out — then attempt separation.

## 3. The five basic separation methods (Gateway Intermediate Workbook / First-Stage Separation)

2. Choose one method and apply it; 'roll out like a log', for example: feel the body rolling sideways on water.

![[sb-separation-3.en.svg|360]]
3. Feel the movement; do not force it. Vibration, a wave or lightness may come.

![[sb-separation-4.en.svg|360]]
4. When the method is done, absorb the REBAL again; come back to the body.

![[sb-separation-5.en.svg|360]]
5. Try one method per session; patiently. The first separation is usually brief.
Every method is attempted in Focus 10/12 with the REBAL in place; at the end the REBAL is reabsorbed.

1. **Roll-out like a log.** Feel your body roll sideways like a log floating on water — without moving a physical muscle. For most people this is the first method that works; it begins as a feeling of "sliding to one side".
2. **Rising like a pole.** Feel your body lift from the head upward like a rigid pole, feet fixed in place.
3. **Backing away.** Slide away from your body backward through the head; the body remains in front of you.
4. **Rising like a gas from the head.** Feel your awareness rise from the crown of your head like a thin gas or vapour.
5. **Floating above.** Feel your body hanging a hand's breadth above itself, face up, floating; then the height increases.

**Screen methods (same workbook):** Watch your body on a screen as a glowing two-dimensional silhouette; change the screen in turn to red, blue, yellow, orange and white. Alternative: feel your body as a statue of translucent material; let your awareness stand outside the statue.

**Rules of practice**
- **One** method per session; do not mix methods.
- **Feel** the movement, don't force it. Vibration, a wave, lightness, "sliding sideways" — all of it is progress.
- Mental tension (frowning, breath-holding) cuts off separation; loosen.
- When fear comes, stop, feel the REBAL, breathe. Fear is a reaction to novelty, not to the method; it fades with repetition.
- Log every attempt in the journal: method, sensations, duration, sticking point.

## 4. Conditions that make separation easier (manual and community experience)

- **Time:** toward morning (after 4–6 hours of sleep) or during an afternoon nap — proximity to REM helps (the WBTB session in Extra Practices exists for this).
- **Body:** a little tired but the mind fresh; neither hungry nor full; bladder empty.
- **Position:** on the back, head slightly raised; for some people lying slightly on the left side.
- **Intent:** clear but light — "today I'm trying the roll-out method", not "I must get out".
- **Expectation load:** the pressure of "I have to get out" is the biggest obstacle; watch the process more than the result.

## 5. Odyssey and the energy body (Focus 12 → 21)

Wave VI Odyssey rebuilds separation in the language of the "energy body":
- **Sensing (Locale 1):** feeling and controlling the energy body — the advanced form of REBAL.
- **Expansion:** extending the energy body beyond the physical body; out of the room, out of the building.
- **Point of Departure:** going "out of phase" — shifting consciousness out of alignment with the physical body and establishing the launch point.
- **Nonphysical Friends:** building trust with "helpers" in energy form.
- **Movement to Locale 2 / Intro Focus 21:** crossing the bridge.
- **Free Flow Journey in Focus 21:** free exploration in the new territory.

Monroe's "Locale" terminology: **Locale I** is the physical world (here-now), **Locale II** the non-physical systems (where thought takes shape instantly), **Locale III** parallel/alternative physical realities (described in the book; no exercise in the series).

## 6. What to expect — and what not to expect — in an out-of-body experience

**Common signs:** vibration/hum, "electric" tingling, loss of weight, swaying, the sense of being "a hand's breadth above" the body, seeing the room from a different angle, hearing sounds (signals, clicks), a sense of racing heart (in reality the heart is usually normal).

**Misconceptions:** an OBE offers no "definitive proof" — the room detail you saw may be wrong; this does not devalue the experience, it makes interpretation careful. The fear "can the silver cord snap?": Monroe never saw permanent harm in any case; return is always possible and easy (think it, and you are there).

**Sleep paralysis:** waking unable to move, pressure on the chest, a sense of a "presence" — this is typical hypnopompic sleep paralysis, harmless, passing within minutes. Method: don't panic, focus on your breath, try to move one finger — or, the other way round, use this state as a gate to separation (the roll-out method is very effective here).

## 7. Safety and ethics

- Out-of-body practice should not be undertaken without a physician's approval in cases of epilepsy, a history of psychosis, severe anxiety or dissociative disorder.
- The request for "protection" (the second part of the affirmation) and the REBAL are the psychological seat belts of the practice; don't skip them.
- Others' privacy: the principle of "permission" in remote viewing and out-of-body practice — don't go where you were not invited.
- Grounding is mandatory; do not drive after deep sessions.
